# Design partner outreach

Goal: 3 to 5 European teams using Kipdeck weekly within 4 to 8 weeks of launch, each willing to share their weekly Numbers table, give a quote and sign a letter of intent for the team tier. Ask 15 to 20 teams to get there. Nothing here has been sent.

## Who to ask

A good design partner has all of these:

- 5 to 50 engineers, AI-native or an agency that ships for clients.
- A senior engineer or tech lead who already runs 3 or more agent sessions in parallel, across 2 or more repositories, ideally with more than one CLI.
- Some of that on a remote Linux box or VM, not only a laptop.
- In the EU or the UK, so a call in the same time zone is easy and EU hosting is a plus, not a question.

Where they come from: people you already know from Vilnius and the Baltic startup scene, and former colleagues. Then people who answer the launch posts, and the waitlist's `5-20` and `21-50` sign-ups.

Name no team anywhere (deck, post, this repository) without its written permission. Keep the list of names in your own tracker, not in git.

## The first message

Short, about them, one ask. Send it from your own account, to one person.

```text
Subject: Agents waiting on your team?

Hi {{FIRST_NAME}},

{{ONE_LINE_ABOUT_WHY_THEM, e.g. "Saw your post about running Claude Code on
three services at once."}}

I've built Kipdeck, an open-source inbox for coding agents: every agent your
team runs (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor) in one list, sorted by what needs a
person, with answer, review and merge one click away. It runs on your own
machine or dev box.

I'm looking for a few teams to use it for a month and tell me what's wrong
with it. In return: setup help, a direct line to me, and the team tier free
for a year once it exists.

Would a 20-minute call next week work? Or try `npx kipdeck --demo` first.

{{YOUR_NAME}}
```

Follow up once, after 5 working days, with one new thing (a short clip of the part they'd care about). No third message.

## The call (20 minutes)

1. Five minutes on them: how many agents, which CLIs, which machine, and what happens today when an agent asks a question. Write down their words.
2. Five minutes of the demo on their kind of problem, not a tour.
3. Five minutes on the ask: install on one dev box this week, use it for real work for four weeks, send the Numbers table each Friday (Copy as Markdown), and a 15-minute call every two weeks.
4. Five minutes on what would make them stop using it.

Then within a day: a written summary of what they said, and an install session if they said yes.

## What counts

| Stage | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Contacted | The first message sent |
| Call | A call held |
| Installed | Kipdeck running on their machine with a real repository |
| Weekly | A Numbers table sent two weeks in a row |
| Partner | Weekly, plus permission to name them, a quote and a signed letter of intent |

## The letter of intent

One page, not binding, in plain words: the team intends to buy the team tier when it's available, at a price to be agreed, for about {{SEATS}} seats; what they need from it (a shared inbox, routing, history); and that you may name them as a design partner. Have it read by someone who knows contracts before the first one goes out. It is a signal for investors, not revenue: never present it as revenue.
