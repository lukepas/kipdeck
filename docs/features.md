# Features

What Kipdeck does. Back to the [README](../README.md).

The inbox at `/` is the product, and the first section here is all most people need. The sections after it describe the whole office in detail: much of that is the 3D Deck at `/deck` and the other [Labs](labs.md) (goals, meetings, voice, ambience, Proof of Merge), each on as Kipdeck ships until an admin switches it off.

## The inbox

- **One list, sorted by what each agent needs from you.** Needs you (a question, a permission, or stuck), To review (finished work, a pull request, failing checks), Working (one line each, what it's doing now) and Ready. The order comes from one ranking, `src/shared/attention.ts`. See [the inbox](inbox.md).
- **One button per row.** Answer opens the agent's live terminal with the cursor in its reply box. Review changes opens its diff, checks and pull request. Fix checks asks it to fix its failing checks. Merge merges its pull request, or its branch on your machine without GitHub. Send back hands the work back with your note.
- **Deploy agent** (or **N**): a project, an agent (Claude Code, Codex and Cursor; OpenCode, Pi, Grok, Muse and DeepSeek Harness in beta) and a task. Each agent works on a branch of its own. `kipdeck attach` moves a session you started in a terminal into the inbox.
- **Notifications** only when an agent starts needing you or has something to review, in the browser, and for the team in Slack or Discord.
- **Shipped today**, with a signed record of every merge and send-back on your machine: which agent and model, the prompt, and who reviewed it.
- **Numbers**: human wait time, changes merged, the merge rate and agent-hours for the last 7 days against the 7 before, merges per day, and the merge rate per agent and model with its N. Copy as Markdown pastes them into an update ([metrics](metrics.md)).
- **Settings** in three panes: Account, Agents and Notifications ([controls](controls.md#the-inbox)).
- **On a phone** the list is the page, and an agent opens over it with large Answer and Merge buttons.
- **Teams**: accounts with single-use invite links, on your machine or a shared dev box ([teams and servers](self-hosting.md)).
- **Six keys**: Ctrl+K, N, Enter, Esc, / and ? ([controls](controls.md)).
- **A demo** with five scripted agents and no model: `kipdeck --demo` from a linked clone, `npx kipdeck --demo` once it is on npm ([the demo](demo.md)).

## The office in detail (much of it the Deck, a lab)

- **A floor per project.** Started inside a git repository, the office makes it the first project, and the home page's setup card offers to clone one from GitHub otherwise (see [Run it](../README.md#run-it)). In the Deck you start on the conn, just north of the Deck lift in the middle of the south curb, and the **Decks** window asks for one: pick one of the repositories your `gh` login can see (or type `owner/name`) and the office clones it into the workspace folder, `~/agent-office/<owner>/<repo>` unless you pick another (**Change folder** right there, for admins). While it clones, its floor in the window says how far along it is (*Downloading 64% · 231 MiB · 1.5 MiB/s*), and you can close the window and carry on: everyone hears when the floor opens. Whoever added it, or an admin, can stop it with the **⏹️** beside it. A clone that hears nothing from GitHub for 3 minutes is stopped as stalled, one that would have to ask something (an ssh host key, a password) fails straight away and says what to run on the office's machine, and restarting the office doesn't stop one. Open the Floors window again with **E** at the elevator or the menu → **Floors**. To go to another floor, click the project name in the top-left corner and pick one: you're there in a blink, standing on the same spot in the office. Each floor has its own desks, workers, issues and PR boards, task queue, services and whiteboard, and its own wall and floor colors, so you always know where you are. You only see and hear the people on your floor. The Floors window shows how many workers are busy or waiting on someone on each floor, and you get a heads-up when a worker on another floor starts waiting. An admin can take a project off the building with **More** > **Remove deck** beside its floor: its workers stop, and everyone on it goes to another floor. Nothing is deleted. The checkout stays on disk with its workers and queue, and adding the same repository again moves back into it (as long as the workspace folder hasn't moved since). That goes for the project the office was started in (`agent-office <dir>`) too: it stays off after a restart, and the office keeps its own data in that folder as before.
- **Walk around.** Use WASD, Space to jump, and drag the mouse to orbit the camera. Everyone on your floor sees everyone else move in real time. The office is evenly lit all day, so a screen across the room is as easy to read at midnight as at noon.
- **A quiet screen.** The office stays in view: the top bar holds the deck you're on, the counters, Mission control, **Units** and the menu (or **Tab**). The menu opens every window (issues, PRs, the queue, services, the whiteboard, the docs, search, voice, screen sharing, settings) and lists what can show on screen: the workers (on by default), who's in the office, spend, Claude limits, the chat and the floor details. Turn each one on or off there, or hide a panel with its **Hide** (x) button. Pin anything you use a lot to the top bar. The choices are kept in your browser. Chat lines fade after a few seconds; hover the chat or press **T** to read back. What needs you shows up on the top bar by itself: the workers waiting on someone (click to go to them, like **N**), the mute button while you're in voice, **Sharing** while you share your screen, and **Update** when there's a new version.
- **Straight in.** The first time you join, nothing stands between you and the office: you get a look dealt at random and nothing is asked. Your name is your account's; on the shared password it's git's `user.name` when the office runs on your own computer, else a made-up one like *Sunny Otter*. The office remembers your name and look in that browser. To pick your skin tone, hair and shirt, open **Your character** in the **Menu**, or click your name under *In the office* (turn on **People** in the menu).
- **The deck.** Every project is the same deck, dressed as a starship's bridge with a forward viewport, side ports and a canopy: the conn where you arrive, a mission table in the middle with the course plotted over it, four pods of consoles facing it, the situation wall curving round its north side (Issues, Queue, Attention, Pull requests, Services), the Proof corner on the west wall, the Review bay in the north-west corner and the Deck lift in the middle of the south curb. Through the glass, space streams past as the ship makes way: faster with each merge, holding station when nothing is deployed, a surge on a merge, a jump to new space when a milestone is done, and now and then a planet, an asteroid field or a comet going by. **Settings > Deck > Ship motion** slows it to Calm or stops it (Off stills the rest of the deck too, as the system's reduce-motion setting does). **Bridge lights** sets Night (low light), Day (high light) or Auto (follows the system), for the HUD, the home page and the sign-in page too, and **Brightness** steps the deck's lights either way. **Quality** (Auto, Low, Medium, High) sets how much of the deck's light and detail is drawn: Auto picks a tier for your graphics and steps down by itself if frames fall behind. Every spot has a cell address (*C4*) from the grid round its edge. Press **G** for the Overview, the whole deck from above. What's where, and the Overview's keys, are in [The deck](deck.md).
- **Take a seat.** Press **E** at the captain's chair on the conn, or at a lounge seat up in the forward lounge, to sit down. Walk off, jump or press **E** again to get up. Everyone on your floor sees you sitting. While someone's sharing their screen, press **E** at the Attention board to watch it full screen.
- **Room to grow.** When a deck needs more consoles, the north wall in its north-east corner comes down. Walk up to the **Room to grow** sign there and press **E**, then **Knock through**: the wall goes, and an overflow bay opens behind it with a pair of consoles. Knock through once more and it goes back another row, for two more. New workers, and the task queue, take its consoles once the deck's are full, before any seat on the Standby bench comes out. The sign moves to the back wall, where **E** can also wall the last row back up, once nobody's working there. Each floor is built out on its own.
- **The Standby bench for the overflow.** There are 16 consoles, and up to 4 more in the overflow bay. Once they're all taken, a seat comes out on the Standby bench along the south curb, with a low stand in front of it. Hire a worker there the same way as at a console, and the next seat comes out, up to 12 along the bench. The task queue seats workers on them too. A seat stays out while a worker sits on it, and the empty one goes away again as soon as a console frees up.

## See what every agent is doing

- **Mission control.** Press **I** (or click the attention chip on the top bar, *2 need you · 1 stuck · 3 to review*) for every hired worker in the building, ranked by how much it needs someone: needs you, stuck (silent for 10 minutes while working, crashed, failing again and again, its worktree deleted, never given a task), to review (done and nobody looked, forgotten after 30 minutes, failing checks, merged), then working and parked. Each row says why in plain words, how long, what it cost and what it's for, with one button for the next step; a worker on another floor takes you there first. Snooze one for a while, shared with everyone. The same ranking orders the Workers panel and the inbox, and a worker that gets stuck dings and notifies. See [Mission control](mission-control.md).
- **A review inbox.** Mission control's **Review** tab (**I**, then **3**) lists everything on every floor that waits for a person's decision, oldest first: finished work nobody looked at, with how much it changed; commits with no pull request yet; the office's pull requests in review, approved and ready to merge, with failing checks or conflicts; pull requests your review is requested on; and merged work whose worker can go home. Each row has its one step: Review changes, Open PR, Merge (the PR window's Merge dialog), Hand back (its *Fix comments & merge*) or Send home.
- **A timeline, and what you missed.** The **Timeline** tab (**4**) says what happened on every floor, newest first: who hired whom for what, who needed input, finished or got stuck, who went home and what it cost, pull requests opened, merged and closed, queue tasks, meetings, and changes to the mission. Filter it by floor, goal or worker; each event opens what it's about. Back after 15 minutes or more, *While you were away* opens once with a one-line summary (*3 PRs merged, 2 workers finished and wait for review, 1 got stuck, Auth rewrite moved from 3/7 to 5/7*) and the events since.
- **Reminders.** What nobody has to answer now but somebody will: a snooze that ran out, a pull request approved and green for an hour and not merged, a paused queue with tasks waiting, a milestone past its due date, a worker asleep for a day with commits nobody pushed, a question waiting over an hour. They sit at the top of the Attention tab, put an amber dot on the attention chip, toast the floor at most once an hour each, and can be snoozed or dismissed.
- **Live status.** Claude Code, Codex, Grok, Muse and Cursor hooks, an OpenCode plugin and DeepSeek Harness's ACP session drive each worker's status: *working*, *needs input* or *done* (Cursor has no hook for its permission prompts, so a Cursor worker waiting on one still shows as *working*; mission control calls it stuck once it has been silent for 2 minutes in the middle of a tool call, *it may be asking permission in its terminal*). When a worker needs a human or has finished, it jumps up and down, and you hear a ding when it's done. Its antenna bulb shows the status from across the room.
- **Task cards.** A card over each worker's head names what it's on ("Fix Login Redirect") and says in one line what it's doing right now. For Claude workers, Claude Haiku writes it from prompts and recent tools through the `claude` CLI. OpenCode, Codex, Grok, Muse, DeepSeek Harness and Cursor use local task summaries and never invoke Claude to name their tasks.
- **Workers act out what they're doing.** A working worker acts out its latest tool call, so you can tell what it's doing from across the room without reading its card. Reading files, it holds up a stack of papers and flips through them. Editing, it hunches over the keys and types flat out. Running tests or a build, it leans back with its hands behind its head. Searching or fetching the web, a globe spins beside its laptop. When its tests or build fail twice in a row, it puts its head in its hands. Waiting on you, it jumps for a couple of seconds, then crosses its arms and taps its foot. When it finishes, it does a little spin and a hop. Claude Code hooks tell a shell command that runs tests from one that only reads, and they report failures. Codex and OpenCode report only the tool's name, so their shell commands are acted out as plain typing.
- **A worker that needs you is hard to miss.** A worker stopped on a question or a permission can't go on until someone answers, so the office makes the most of it. Who counts is Mission control's *Needs you* level (see [Mission control](mission-control.md)), so a worker you snoozed there stays quiet: no banner, beacon, alarm or desktop notification, and **N** and its count skip it. In the room, a red pin with a **!** floats over its card, a pillar of red light stands over its desk and rings spread out over the floor around it, all of which you can see from the far side of the office; the light fades as you walk up, so it doesn't get between you and the laptop. Its bubble turns red and says **NEEDS YOU**. On screen, a red banner under the top bar says who needs you, what it's asking, which floor when it isn't yours, and for how long, with **+2 more** when others are asking too. Someone on your floor comes first, as with **N**. Click it to be put at that desk (or taken to that floor), or **Snooze** it to put it away until someone else asks. The moment a worker on your floor starts asking, the edge of the screen flashes and the needs-you cue plays: a breath as the comm opens and two rising chimes. Under *When a unit needs you*, pick **Once**, **Keep reminding me** (it plays again, softly, every 30 seconds until someone opens that worker's terminal) or **Off**. Sound is on from your first click or key (Shift+M or Settings > *Sound & voice* turns it off). The cues are the loudest thing the deck plays and everything else steps back while anyone waits: a stuck unit has two low ticks, a unit ready for review one soft tone, and a merge proven on chain a low thunk and a tick. Under them are the deck's own effects and its ambience, each with a level of its own in the mixer (see [Design system](design.md#sound)). The banner is the one thing on screen that stays up for it, and it holds still once it's in; the attention chip on the top bar and the **N** button only turn red. With *reduce motion* on in your system, the flash is a single fade. One that needs you on another floor is on the banner and the count by the project name, with no toast or cue of its own.
- **The Units rail.** The rail down the left lists every unit on the deck, grouped by state, the ones that need someone first, with why (*needs input for 4 min*, *working but silent for 12 min*) or its status, its task, its desk and its time on task; click one to open its terminal. One that needs you is in red, with what it's asking on a line of its own. It's on screen from the start, and the menu hides it.
- **Next worker that needs you.** Press **N** to go straight to the first worker waiting on someone (it needs input, or it's done and nobody has looked), standing behind it and looking at its laptop. Press **N** again for the next one, and after the last you start over: the ones that need input come first, then the ones that are done, and whoever has waited longest is first among each. A worker that needs you on another floor comes before one on yours that's only done. A pin at the edge of the screen points the way to each waiting worker you can't see, red for *needs input* and green for *done*, and the Workers panel's button counts them (`2 need you · 1 done`, in red and pulsing while anyone needs you; click it to do the same as **N**). With nobody left waiting on your floor, **N** takes you to the next floor's, to that worker's desk.
- **Notifications.** You don't have to be in the office tab. The tab title counts the workers that need someone on every floor, like `(2) acme-shop - Kipdeck`, the same count as the home page's pulse. Allow desktop notifications (the office asks when you first hire a worker, or turn them on under **Menu > Deck > Settings** > *Notifications*) and a worker that needs you, finishes or gets stuck while you're in another tab or app pops one up (*Byte needs you*, with what it's asking; that one stays up until it's dealt with). Click it to go straight to that worker: for one that needs you or is done, the office comes to the front with you at its desk and its terminal open. For the whole team, paste a Slack or Discord incoming webhook under **Menu > Deck > Settings** > *Team notifications* (or start with `--webhook <url>`), and the office posts to that channel when a worker has needed input, been done or been stuck for a few seconds with nobody at its terminal, and once when a worker has waited on an answer for over an hour. Only admins set, test or remove the webhook; everyone else sees where it posts.
- **Shared terminals.** Press **E** at an occupied desk to open the real terminal (a PTY, over WebSockets). Several people can type into the same session at once, and anyone who joins late gets the full scrollback. The terminal's header shows a face for everyone who has it open, and a line like *Sam is typing…* while someone else types, so you don't talk over each other. The Esc key leaves the terminal, so to send the program an Esc (to close a menu like Claude's `/skills`, or to interrupt it) press **Ctrl+[** or click **⎋ Esc** in the header. Drag a screenshot (or any file) onto an open terminal, or paste a screenshot into it, and the office keeps a copy on its own machine and types its path, as a terminal does with a file dragged into it: Claude Code and Codex attach the picture as *[Image #1]*. The copies are kept in `.agent-office/drops/` until the worker goes home.
- **Dictation.** Talk to a worker instead of typing. In a worker's terminal, or any box you write a prompt in (hiring, **P** at a desk, a board agent, an issue's *Ask a worker*, the task queue, a meeting), hold **Ctrl+Space** or the **Dictate** button and say it. The words show as they're heard and are typed in at the cursor when you let go; a quick tap instead of a hold leaves it listening, hands free, until the next tap. Nothing is sent for you: read it over, fix what it misheard, and press Enter yourself. There's nothing to install, because the listening is your browser's own speech recognition: Chrome, Edge and Safari have one, and where a browser doesn't (Firefox) there's no . The office never gets the audio, but the browser's maker does: Chrome and Edge send it to their speech service, unless the browser already has its on-device model for your language, which is used instead. The office asks the browser about that model when you first point at or press the mic, not when a window opens. It listens for your browser's language. Like voice, it needs HTTPS or localhost. In voice with an open mic, you're muted while you dictate. On a Mac with more than one keyboard language, Ctrl+Space switches between them, so use the there. In the inbox the is on the prompt box under the terminal.
- **Web page tabs.** Click **+ Web page** on a terminal to pin a web page open beside it, like a linked ChatGPT conversation or docs for the task at hand: give it a name and an https address and it opens in its own tab next to **Terminal**. Only https pages on other sites open there, never the office's own pages or http ones (see [security](security.md)). Switch between tabs without losing your place in either, and close a web page's tab with the **×** on it. Plenty of sites won't show inside another page (ChatGPT is one): **↗ New tab** opens the page you're on in a browser tab of its own. The terminal keeps running behind the scenes while you're looking at a web page tab. Tabs are remembered for that worker until you reload the page.
- **Search.** Press **/** (or **Search** in the menu) to search the chat and every worker's terminal at once, including what they showed before the office restarted. Click a terminal line to open that terminal scrolled to it, with the line lit up.
- **Command palette.** Press **Ctrl+K** (**⌘K** on a Mac) anywhere in the office to find anything on your floor by typing a few letters: workers (by name, task card or desk), issues, pull requests, services, the boards, teammates, and actions (hire a worker, open the task queue, settings, invite). Words, word starts and scattered letters all match, best first, so "login" finds the worker whose card says *Fix Login Redirect*. **↑ ↓** choose and **Enter** opens it the way clicking it in the office does: a worker's terminal, a PR's window, a service in a new tab. **Shift+Enter** walks your character over to it first and opens it when you get there (take a step yourself to call it off). **Esc** or **Ctrl+K** again puts it away. It doesn't open while you're typing in a terminal or a text box, where Ctrl+K is the terminal's own (cut to the end of the line).
- **Cost per worker.** Claude Code workers show tracked session cost and tokens. OpenCode workers show tokens and model/provider-reported cost estimates, including child-session usage, with a cache/reasoning breakdown in tooltips. These estimates are not billing; missing cost is shown as unavailable. Codex workers show the root session's recorded tokens, including cache and reasoning breakdowns; cost and API-call count are unavailable. DeepSeek Harness workers show what is in each session's context window (and the window's size) from ACP's usage updates, plus a cost only when the harness reports one. Today's and all-time totals, `--budget` warnings and `--budget-pause` use tracked Claude Code spend only; they cannot cap OpenCode, Codex or DeepSeek Harness spending. Once the tracked budget is spent, the hiring pause applies to all new workers. OpenCode and Codex session snapshots and Claude totals survive restarts. Current-desk totals disappear when their workers are sent home.
- **Capacity panel and worker limit.** The capacity panel at the head of the Proof corner, on the west wall, shows how busy the office's machine is: CPU and memory right now, the last five minutes of each, and how many workers the office runs. Start with `--max-workers <n>`, or have an admin set a limit under **Menu > Deck > Settings** > *Worker limit*, and the office runs at most that many workers at once across every floor (shells and board agents count too). Hiring past it is refused with a message saying why, and the task queue waits for room instead of failing its tasks. In the office, the limit can be lowered but never raised past `--max-workers`. When memory is 90% used, or the CPU has been 90% busy for half a minute, the panel says the machine is under pressure, the hint at an empty desk says so, and the hire dialog warns you before you hire.
- **Plan limits.** Turn on **⏳ Claude limits** in the menu and a meter shows how much of your Claude plan's 5-hour session and week is used, plus any per-model weekly limit (like Fable's), and when each one starts over. These are the same numbers Claude Code's `/usage` shows, on every floor: for your own Claude sign-in once you have one (see **Your own Claude and GitHub** below), else for the one the office's machine is signed in to. While anyone is in the office, it asks `claude` for them every two minutes, which starts no conversation and costs nothing. Click the meter to read it again. It stays hidden when Claude runs on an API key instead of a plan, and it doesn't count OpenCode, Codex, Grok, Muse, DeepSeek Harness or Pi.
- **The home page.** `/` is [the inbox](inbox.md), on a computer or a phone: every agent on every project in four sections (Needs you, To review, Working, Ready) in the same order as Mission control, each row with the agent's mark, its task, one status line (what it asks, what it changed, what it's doing), how long it has waited, a wait bar while it waits on you, and a button only where there's a decision (Answer, Review changes, Fix checks, Merge, Send back or Resume). The selected agent sits beside the list with its live terminal (an agent's question as a card over it, with one reply box and its choices as buttons), its diff with Merge and Send back, and its log; on a wide screen the oldest agent waiting on you opens there by itself. The top bar's pulse says who is waiting on you now, the median wait today and what merged. **Deploy agent** (or N) starts one on a branch named after its task; a merge lands in Shipped today, written to a signed local log, and the next agent that needs you is selected. A three-step checklist sits under the list until done (none in the demo). The avatar menu has Numbers, Settings, Help and Sign out; the GitHub boards, the Task queue and Mission control are a lab, and [Labs](labs.md) is in Settings and Ctrl+K, which also finds any agent. The page loads no three.js and opens its windows only when first wanted, so it draws after about 175 kB; `tests/home-budget.test.ts` holds it there. The old `/lite` address lands here, and a browser that can't draw the 3D Deck is sent back here. The tab title counts who's waiting, and with notifications on an agent that needs you pops one up and buzzes the phone. With Deck on in Labs, the top bar links to `/deck` and the deck plan fills the pane while no agent is selected. Teammates see you as on the home page, not standing anywhere in the bridge.
- **Survives restarts.** Workers are saved to disk with their agent choice and session ID, and their terminals run in a small process of their own. When the server restarts (a dev-server reload, a `kill` of its process), the agents keep working through it, and the new server picks every terminal back up, scrollback and all. Open terminals reconnect by themselves. Ctrl+C stops the workers; the next start wakes them back up in their sessions, and **R** resumes the Claude Code, OpenCode, Codex, Grok, Muse, DeepSeek Harness, Pi or Cursor session of one that exited. DeepSeek Harness workers are the exception to keeping working through a restart: their ACP session is a child of the office process, so it stops with the office and comes back *offline*, resumable with **R**. When a restart does take a worker's terminal with it (the machine rebooted, the terminal process died or was replaced by a newer one), a worker that was in the middle of something is resumed with *continue*, so it carries on by itself. The chat and each worker's last 3,000 lines of terminal are kept too, so after a restart (or a crash) you can still scroll back through what the workers printed and what everyone said.

## Hand work out

- **Hire workers.** Walk up to an empty desk and press **E** to choose Claude Code, OpenCode, Codex, Grok, Muse, DeepSeek Harness, Pi or Cursor, or press **P** to write a task first. A little worker sits down, a laptop opens, and the agent's live screen appears on it. Choose the agent for each queue task too.
- **Tags by the consoles.** Press **L** at any console, empty or not, to stencil a tag on the floor by it: *Operations*, *Code cleanup*, *Frontend*, whatever that console is for. Type up to 32 characters (or pick one of the ideas), choose one of seven colors and press **Enter**. It lies on the console's table side, the right way up from its stool, with its color as a stripe; nothing hangs between the table and the consoles. Everyone on the floor sees it, the hint at the desk names it, and it stays up across restarts. **L** again changes it or takes it down. Each floor has its own.
- **Shared shells.** Press **B** at an empty desk to open a plain login shell for dev servers, git or tests. It's shared the same way as a Claude terminal.
- **Isolated branches.** When you hire with a task, you can tick *own git worktree*. The worker then gets its own `office/<name>` branch under `.agent-office/worktrees/`, so parallel workers never share a checkout. The branch starts from the latest on GitHub, pull requests merged since anyone last pulled included, so a task never undoes the one before it. When you send that worker home you choose whether to keep the worktree and branch, delete the worktree, or delete both, and the office warns you first about uncommitted changes or commits no remote has. `kipdeck prune` (`agent-office prune` still works) clears out whatever was kept, once it is safe. If a worker's worktree is deleted outside the office (`rm -rf`, `git worktree remove`, a cleanup script), the worker doesn't keep failing to start: it waits at its desk under a *worktree deleted* bubble. **E** there (or clicking it in the **Workers** list) says what's left and offers **Rebuild worktree**, which checks its branch out again in the same place (origin's copy when only the pushed one is left, or a fresh branch from where it started when the branch is gone too) and lets it carry on its conversation, **Rebuild all** for every worker on the floor that lost one, or **Send home…**. Under Settings > Agents > *After a merge*, anyone can have them go home by themselves once their pull request merges: as soon as one isn't working or waiting on you and nobody has its terminal open, it leaves and its worktree and branch are deleted. Uncommitted changes, or commits that aren't on GitHub, keep the worktree, as when you send it home. It's off until someone turns it on, and turning it on sends home the ones already merged.
- **One task across several projects.** Many systems are split over repositories (an api, an admin, a web app), and one change often needs two or three of them. When you hire a worker in its own worktree, **Also work in** lists the building's other floors: pick the projects the task spans (in the hire dialog, or in **Hand to a worker** / **Ask a worker** from the boards). The worker starts in a workspace folder with a worktree of each project in it, all on the same `office/<name>` branch, and a short brief (as `CLAUDE.md` and `AGENTS.md`) saying which folder is which. It works across them, tests them together and commits in each. **O** then opens a pull request in every project it committed to, and each description lists all of them, so they're reviewed and merged as one change. The issue the task came from is closed by the home floor's PR and linked from the others. Press **O** again for each project's PR, with a button to open the ones still missing. The Changes window has a tab per project, sending the worker home checks and deletes every worktree, and *After a merge* waits until the PRs in every project have merged. The brief can be rewritten in Settings like the office's other prompts.
- **Carry an issue to a desk.** On the issues board, point at a note (the crosshair in first person, the mouse in third) and press **E** or click to take its card off the board (**O** opens it instead); **Pick it up** in an issue's window does the same. You carry the card across the office, where everyone on the floor sees it (and sees it missing from the board): press **E** at an empty desk to hire a worker for it, at a worker's desk to hand it to that worker, or at the task queue to queue it. However a worker gets an issue (**Hand to a worker**, its card, or the queue seating one for it), the issue moves to *In progress* and its note comes off the wall the moment the worker has it, for everyone on the floor. The office assigns it on GitHub behind that, which is what keeps it there; if GitHub won't assign it, the card goes back and you're told why. **Q** puts the card back.
- **Task queue.** The second panel of the situation wall, between Issues and the Attention board, and **Task queue** in the menu. Click **Add to queue** on any issue, or type a free-text task, and walk away. Whenever a desk is free and fewer than *workers at once* of its tasks are running (3 by default; 0 pauses the queue), the next task gets a fresh worker in its own git worktree, at a free console in the pod working toward its goal when it has one (see [Pods and goals](deck.md#pods-and-goals)). Workers you hire yourself don't count against it; the office's worker limit, if there is one, counts everyone. The issue moves to *In progress* on the issues board as its worker sits down (and is assigned on GitHub), and the pull request is linked on the whiteboard as soon as it shows up. Finished workers stay at their desks so you can read their terminals, until the queue needs the desk for the next task. The queue survives restarts: a task whose worker carries on through the restart stays running and keeps its slot, and one whose worker didn't come back is marked as stopped so you can requeue it.
- **Ask a worker about anything on a board.** Every issue and PR has **Ask a worker…**: type your own prompt, and the worker gets it along with which issue or PR it's about. Send it to a new worker at a free desk, or to one already sitting at a desk.
- **Board agents.** An agent stands at a little kiosk beside each of the issues board, the PR board and the task queue. Walk up and press **E**, type what you want, and it runs as a Claude Code (or your configured) agent that already knows its job. The issues agent files, finds, triages, labels and closes issues with `gh`. The PR agent sums up, reviews, comments on and merges pull requests. The queue agent turns what you ask for into tasks on the queue, one per piece of work, and can tell you what's queued or take tasks off. It never does the work itself, not even a one-line fix: it queues it and tells you what it queued. The first prompt hires it; after that your prompts go into the same session, so you can follow up, and a sleeping one wakes up with your prompt. Press **O** there to watch its terminal and **X** to send it home, and the next prompt starts a fresh one. The agents work in the floor's main checkout, so they're told not to change code there: work that needs code changed goes on the queue, where a fresh worker does it in its own worktree.
- **Agents that manage agents.** Any worker can see and run the office's other workers. Ask one to "send everyone whose PR merged home", "hire someone to fix the flaky test" or "tell Mochi to rebase", and it can. Claude Code, Codex and OpenCode workers get an `agent-office` MCP server with six tools: `list_workers` (who's at which desk, their status and task, their branch, where their pull request stands, and what needs someone and why, so a managing agent can find the stuck ones), `hire_worker` (with an optional milestone, `goal`), `get_mission` (the floor's mission and milestones, and the caller's own), `send_home`, `tell_worker` and `link_pr`. Every worker, whichever agent it runs, also has the same thing as the `office-workers` command (`office-workers list`, `office-workers mission`, `office-workers home --merged`, and so on). Sending home works as it does from a desk: each worker's worktree and branch are deleted unless they hold work that isn't on GitHub (what a merged PR delivered counts as on GitHub), and the answer says what was kept and why. Everyone on the floor sees a toast saying which worker did what, and nobody can send themselves home.
- **Whose pull request is whose.** A worker's desk shows where its pull request stands (open, or merged and free to go home) rather than just *ready* or *done*. The office knows the pull request when it was opened from the desk (**O**), from the branch the office gave the worker, or by a queue task. A Claude Code worker that runs `gh pr create` itself is linked to what that opened too, whatever branch it pushed, which is how a worker hired without its own worktree gets one. For the ones it can't see (another agent that opened its own from a branch of its own, or one opened by hand), ask any worker to "link the PRs to the workers that opened them": it matches them up and says so with `link_pr` (`office-workers pr 12 --worker Mochi`, or `--none` to take a wrong one off).
- **The Review bay.** In the north-west corner there's the Review bay, smoked glass round a small table. Press **E** at it (or **Review bay** in the menu, which sits on the top bar while a meeting is on), or **Meeting…** on an issue or **Review panel…** on a PR, to call a meeting: 2 to 5 workers step out of the elevator one after another, walk over and sit down round the table, each with a role you can rename, and work through one question or task together in a pattern. **Debate**: everyone proposes, then critiques the others, and the head of the table writes the decision. **Lead & team**: the lead splits the task into a part for each of the others, they each do theirs, and the lead merges the work. **Map-reduce**: the same task over a list of parts (files, modules, issues) shared out between the mappers, then one worker combines what they found. **Red / blue**: red attacks the change (bugs, security holes, edge cases) and blue fixes what holds up, round after round, until red finds nothing more. **Review panel**: reviewers each read a PR through their own lens (correctness, security, performance...), and the head of the table merges them into one review, every finding tagged with its lens, which the office posts on the PR. Every meeting declares its output file up front (`docs/decisions/....md`, a review...) and ends when that file is written: the office commits it on the meeting's own branch (**Open PR** in the meeting window opens a pull request for it). Meetings are bounded by a round limit per pattern, and have no token limit. You pick the round limit for a debate (2 to 4) or red / blue (1 to 5). The other patterns always run the same rounds, so the form says so in place of a limit to set (**3 rounds · fixed by the Lead & team workflow**) and names them: planning, execution and merge for lead & team, map and reduce for map-reduce, the reviews and the combined review for a review panel. When a worker won't write its part, or when someone is sent home, the meeting stops and says why; **Stop meeting** stops one by hand. The sign on the door shows the pattern, the round, and the tokens and cost so far, and a one-line summary once it's over; the board on the east wall shows the output as it's written, and the card over each worker says whose turn it is and which round. The workers stay at the table so you can read their terminals until **Clear the room** or the next meeting sends them home.
- **Sent home with a box.** A worker you send home (**X** at its desk) doesn't just vanish. Its light goes out, its face falls, and its things go into a cardboard box while its laptop snaps shut; then it shrinks away where it sat. The desk is free once it's gone, so you can hire someone new straight away.

## Review what comes back

- **Changes at the desk.** Press **C** at an occupied desk (or click **Changes** in its terminal) to see what the worker changed: the changed files and their diff against the branch the office was opened on, refreshed every couple of seconds while it works. From there you can commit, discard, or push the branch and open a pull request.
- **One-click PRs.** When a worktree worker is done, press **O** at its desk. The office pushes the branch and opens a pull request with a title and body drafted from the task (work handed over from the issues board gets a `Closes #n`). The PR shows up on the PR board right away, tagged with the worker's desk, and **Go to desk** takes you there. Press **O** again to see the PR.
- **Issues board.** A tack board shows GitHub issues in *Open*, *In progress* and *Closed*. The notes pinned to the board on the wall are the *Open* ones, the issues nobody has started on: one that moves to *In progress* comes off the wall, and you find it in the board's window. Click a column's header to filter it by label: pick one or more and the column shows only the cards with any of them. Your filters stay put for each floor, in your browser. Or type in the box under a column's header to narrow it to the cards whose titles have every word you type; it works along with the label filter, **Clear** clears it, and the boxes start empty each time you open the board. Click an issue to read it and its comments, or add a comment of your own (it posts as the GitHub account you signed in to in **Your sign-ins**, or on the office password as the one `gh` is signed in to on the server; the window names it), then choose **Hand to a worker** to seat a worker with a ready-made prompt (or give it to one who's already there), or **Add to queue**. Either way the worker takes the issue: it moves to *In progress* at once and is assigned on GitHub. **Close issue…** closes it as completed or not planned, with an optional comment, and takes it off the queue if it was waiting there. **Edit** next to an issue's labels (**Add labels** when it has none), or the that shows when you hover its card on the board, picks its labels from the repo's own: tick them on and off, filter by typing (**Enter** ticks the first match), then **Save labels**. An *in progress* (or *doing*, *wip*) label moves the issue to *In progress*.
- **PR board.** A second tack board shows pull requests in *Draft*, *In review*, *Approved*, *Merged* and *Closed*, with CI status and diff size. Its column headers filter by label too, and the box under each header by title. Click a PR to read its description, comments, reviews and checks, and comment on it. **Files changed** shows the diff, with the files listed on the left as a folder tree or a flat list, and review comments under the lines they're on. Tick a file **Reviewed** (or press **V**; **J**/**K** step through files) and it folds away. Your ticks are kept in your browser, and a file you ticked that changes again is flagged. **Merge…** squashes, merges or rebases it, and can have GitHub merge it once its checks pass. **Close PR…** closes it without merging, with an optional comment, and can delete its branch. **Review** and **Fix comments & merge** hand the PR to a worker; the second one addresses the review comments, gets the checks green and merges. When the PR conflicts with its base, that button becomes **Fix conflicts & merge**: a new worker is picked by default, merges the base in, resolves the conflicts and merges. Its labels change the same way as an issue's: **Edit** in the PR window, or the on its card.
- **Services board.** When a worker starts a web server (`npm run dev`, a preview build, `python -m http.server`), it appears within a few seconds on the **Services** board, the east end of the situation wall, and is also in the menu. Each entry shows the worker, its branch and the page's title. Point at a row on the board and press **E** to watch it live on the **service monitor** on the east wall: walk up and the page itself is on the screen, **E** there lets you click into it, **O** opens it full screen ([The service monitor](deck.md#the-service-monitor)). In the board's window (**O** at the board, or **E** off its rows), click a row to copy one command that opens that server on your own computer. Or run `agent-office tunnel` on your computer once and leave it running: every server a worker starts opens there on the same port, by itself, and closes when the worker stops it ([Workers' servers on your own computer](tunnel.md)). Hire a worker with its own worktree, ask it to run the dev server, and your designer can review the branch in their own browser.

## Stay on mission

- **A mission per floor.** Each floor says what it's for: a mission statement and up to 12 milestones, one of them active, each with the issues it covers. Edit them in place in Mission control's **Goals** tab (**I**, then **2**); an admin can lock them. The mission strip under the floor's name shows the statement, the active milestone's progress and how many workers aren't linked to anything. Every new worker gets the mission before its first prompt, framed as context from the team, and every worker and queue task is linked to a milestone or an issue (**For goal** in the hire and queue forms), with each milestone's issues closed, open pull requests, workers, spend and time adding up. See [Mission control](mission-control.md).
- **Milestones.** When a pull request merges, however it merged (from the PR window, by a worker's `gh pr merge`, by auto-merge or on GitHub itself), everyone on the floor gets a toast saying which one and who merged it, and a violet pulse runs from the unit's console to the mission table (the merge beat, see [The deck](deck.md#the-merge-beat)). When the last task on the task queue gets done, the floor gets a toast too. Either one raises a desktop notification while the office is in another tab, so nobody misses the work landing.
- **A docs rack of the project's docs.** A rack stands against the east wall. Press **E** at it (or **Docs** in the menu) to read every Markdown file in the project: the README, the docs folder, specs, each package's notes. Type in the filter box to narrow the list down. The letters only need to be in order, so `gs` finds `getting-started.md`, and each extra word narrows it again. Use **↑ ↓** and **Enter** to open one, and **Page Up/Down** to read on without leaving the box. Docs look the way GitHub shows them, with tables, code, callouts, task lists and the project's own pictures. A link to another doc opens it right there, **Contents** jumps to any heading, and the shelf reopens on the doc you last read. Everyone else sees what you're reading under your name tag, and the hint at the shelf says who else on the floor is reading.
- **A planning board to sketch on together.** A board hangs on the west wall, between the Review bay and the Proof corner. Press **E** at it (or **Whiteboard** in the menu) and [Excalidraw](https://excalidraw.com) opens, with shapes, arrows, freehand, text and pasted pictures. Everyone on the floor who opens it draws on the same board at once: you see their strokes as they draw, their cursors with their names, and what they have selected. The top of the window shows who's drawing, and so does the hint at the board. Whatever is drawn shows on the whiteboard in the office for everyone walking past, and stays there across restarts. Each floor has its own. Press Esc once to finish what you're doing and again to close it.
- **See what teammates are up to.** A line under each person's name tag says what they have open (*in Pixel's terminal*, *reading PR #12*, *at the issues board*) or where they are (*in the captain's chair*, *in the review bay*, *in the overflow bay*). The same line shows under their name in the sidebar. Click someone there to walk over to them, going to their floor first if they're on another one. Any key of your own takes over.

## Work together

- **Voice.** Browser-to-browser WebRTC voice. Volume depends on how close you stand, but people are never fully silent. **V** joins, and once you're in, **V** is push to talk: hold it to talk, and you're muted when you let go. **M** mutes and unmutes. Pick **Push to talk** under **Menu > Deck > Settings** > *Voice chat* to join muted, or keep an open mic. Leave voice from the menu.
- **Screen sharing.** Your screen appears on the Attention board for everyone while you share, and there's a full-screen viewer.
- **Office sounds.** Busy workers clatter away at their keyboards, footsteps pad past, a desk phone rings now and then, and a ding says a worker is done or needs input. It's all synthesized in the browser and placed where it happens, so it gets louder as you walk closer. Turn it down or mute it under **Menu > Deck > Settings** > *Sound & voice*, which also covers the worker dings but not voice chat.
- **An account for everyone.** Open **Accounts** from the menu and make an invite link. Whoever opens it picks a password and gets an account in their own name. That name is the one on their character, in chat and on every terminal they type into (**⌨️** in the terminal header shows who typed last), and nobody else can take it. Admins see everyone's accounts there, can make someone an admin, and can revoke an account, which signs that person out at once. The shared office password keeps working alongside the accounts until an admin switches it off. Sessions are signed cookies, and login attempts are rate limited.
- **Your own Claude and GitHub.** With an account, the workers you hire run on your own Claude plan, and the office acts on GitHub as you: comments, merges, labels, claimed issues, commits, pushes and pull requests show up under your name, with your git name and GitHub noreply email. The first time you come in, **Your sign-ins** opens (it's in the menu too). **Sign in with Claude** gives you Claude's sign-in page and takes back the code it shows; **Sign in with GitHub** shows a one-time code for github.com/login/device. Or paste a token from `claude setup-token` (or an API key), or a GitHub token. A shell you open at a desk runs as you, so `claude auth login` and `gh auth login` typed there sign you in too. Hiring a Claude worker, queueing a task, calling a meeting or asking a board agent needs your own Claude sign-in first; without one, the window opens and says why. A worker keeps running as whoever hired it: a queued task as whoever queued it, a meeting's workers as whoever called it. Admins can pick the office machine's own sign-ins instead. Everyone's sign-ins are kept apart, so any number of people can be signed in to different accounts at once. Revoking an account signs it out and deletes its sign-ins. On the office password, with no account, everything runs on the machine's own `claude` and `gh` as before.

## What's gone

Earlier versions had a lot of scenery and games around the office: a castle map and custom JSON maps, a rooftop bar and DJ, darts, axes, golf, smoke breaks, a balcony, a garage with drivable cars and a scenic loop, the street and city outside, a day and night sky with live weather, Halloween and Christmas themes, an office dog, an arcade cabinet, Minesweeper, a jukebox, a coffee machine, a boss's loft with a telescope, a ladder and fire poles between floors, a merge gong with confetti, pictures on the walls, emotes and basketball. They're gone so the office stays about the work. Their old state files (`map.json`, `maps/`, `theme.json`, `arcade.json`, and per floor `decor.json`, `dog.json`, `jail.json` and `jukebox.json` under `.agent-office/`) are left on disk untouched and no longer read; delete them whenever you like.
