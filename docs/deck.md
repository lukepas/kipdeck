# The deck

The deck is the room drawn by Kipdeck's 3D Deck, a lab that shows the same agents as the inbox.

Back to the [README](../README.md).

Every project is a deck, and the room on it is the bridge of a ship under way: one operations floor inside a hull, the same on every project, built as a command amphitheatre. The mission table stands in a pit at deck level; south of it the deck steps up in two curved tiers of consoles to the captain's raised dais; the situation arc hangs over the north side, in front of the bow. It is built so that a glance from the captain's chair (or from the Overview above it) tells you which units need a person, which are stuck and which are only at work. The plan is `src/shared/layout.ts`, which the server checks seats against and the 2D view reads too, so the 3D deck and the rest never drift apart.

## What's where

The deck is a 32 m square. North is the bow and the situation arc's side; the Deck lift stands in the middle of the south curb, its portal facing north, with the dais just north of it. You arrive on the dais at the captain's right hand, looking down over the tiers and the pit to the Attention board and the bow. The floor's height anywhere is `heightAt(x, z)` in `src/shared/amphitheater.ts` (re-exported by `layout.ts`), which walking, the units, the light and the tests all read.

### The service monitor

A screen flush on the east wall (`SERVICE_MONITOR` in `src/shared/wall-screens.ts`, built by `src/client/features/monitor/`), 1.4 by 0.875 m in a graphite bezel with a ship-cyan hairline under it, shows the live page of a service a unit is running: the first the office lists, until you point at a row of the Services board and press E (the row is outlined; O there opens the board's window), or press C at the monitor for the next one.

- **Live, up close.** Walk within 8.5 m of it, facing it, and the page itself is laid over the screen: a real, sandboxed web page placed in 3D by three's CSS3DRenderer, over the canvas and under the HUD and every window. It is only there while the screen is wholly in view and nothing of the deck stands in front of it (rays to its corners a few times a second); at a slant, past 9.5 m, behind something, under a window or in the Overview it goes and the screen's card shows: the service's name and port, whose it is, its command and its keys, or *No service running*. From across the deck the card shows how many services run, big.
- **Using it.** E at the monitor gives the page the mouse: mouse-look lets go cleanly and you click and type into it. Esc, E, the toolbar's Back or a click on the deck takes you back into mouse-look. A page you clicked into keeps Esc to itself, so the deck takes the keyboard back the moment the pointer is off the page. Its toolbar has Reload, Next, Full screen and Open in a tab; R, C and O do the same from the deck.
- **Full screen.** O opens the page in a window with its close button top right and Esc to close it, straight back into mouse-look.
- **What it loads.** Only the link the Services board opens for a port the office lists (`src/shared/service-link.ts`): `localhost:<port>` through a tunnel, the worker's own server when the office runs on this computer, or the service's own link on the office's Tailscale name. Never an address typed in and never the office's own origin; see [Security](security.md#the-service-monitor). Where this page can't frame it (an office on another computer reached by its domain), at Low quality or without CSS 3D, the card has an Open button: E opens it full screen, or in a tab.
- **Cost.** One more screen in the frame; the page is drawn by the browser, not the GPU's scene. Nothing runs for it while the tab is hidden, and a page left unseen for 45 s is unloaded.

### Cell addresses

A grid of columns every 4 m is stencilled round the deck's edge: letters A to H west to east, numbers 1 to 8 north to south. Every spot is in a cell, like `C4`, and the Attention board names each unit's cell. `cellOf(x, z)` in `src/shared/layout.ts` works one out.

### Pods and goals

A pod takes on the goal most of the units at its consoles work toward. The task queue seats a new unit with a goal in that goal's pod while it has a free console, then in a pod nobody has claimed yet, then wherever is free (`src/shared/pods.ts`). A unit whose goal changes keeps its console.

Each pod is a zone you can read from the Overview without reading a unit's label (`src/client/features/pods/`):

- **Its colour is its goal.** The pod's slice of its tier, from the inner edge to the outer one and half a console past its end consoles, is washed in its goal's hue at 55% with a brighter line round it at 90%, so it reads as the room's floor colour from the Overview. All four zones draw as one fill and one outline, and all four labels as one mesh on one canvas: three draws for the pods. The hues are six muted colours from cool to warm (cyan-blue, violet, teal, indigo, rose, sand; `--pod-1` to `--pod-6` in `src/client/styles/tokens.css`, the same values as `src/shared/podhue.ts`), chosen to stay well away from the state colours, so a zone never looks like an alert. Each goal keeps the same hue wherever it shows up, two goals on a deck never share one, two pods on the same goal share it, and a pod with no goal is the neutral slate. When a pod's goal changes, its colour cross-fades over 500 ms.
- **Its ground label sums it up.** A label lies flat on the deck beside its own pod, turned to read the right way up from the Overview's opening view: A's and B's (the back tier's) just outside the tiers' outer rail, off the middle of their own zone; D's and C's (the front tier's, walled in by the back one) past the tiers' west and east ends, the open deck nearest them. Each lies nearer its own pod's zone than any other's. It has the pod's letter, a bar in its goal's hue, a counts line in bold first, and under it the goal (cut to 34 characters; with no goal the counts stand alone in the middle, since the deck's mission is on the table): the counts say what waits on you first, then what's at work, as in `1 needs you · 1 stuck · 3 working`, always at one size: when a busy pod's line wouldn't fit the label, the calm counts at its end give way to `+N` (the units they hold), so the busiest pod never reads smallest. Needs you is Signal orange, stuck red, to review yellow and the rest grey; with nothing waiting and nothing at work it says `idle`, and snoozed units are left out, as the top bar's chip leaves them out. The counts come from the same ranking as everything else (`src/shared/attention.ts`), looked at twice a second, and a label is painted again only when its words change. A count that changes rolls, the old number sliding up and out as the new one comes in (220 ms; at once with less motion). From the Overview it keeps about the same size on screen whatever the zoom, as the callouts do: it is sized so its counts line reads about 11 px (up to 2.5 times zoomed out, down to 0.6 zoomed in), growing in with the move up rather than jumping on the frame it lands, and every trip up into the Overview frames each label whole, clear of the rail and the bottom bar. One that the Units rail would cover slides along its pod's front edge until it's clear, and one still under it hides, so no cut-off word pokes out from under it. From the Overview it draws over any rail or wall in front of it, but under the units' callouts, which keep off its words (`src/client/features/workers/declutter.ts`).
- **Walking, it gets out of your way.** The zones always show. A label fades out as you come within 5 m of it and is gone inside 4 m, so you never stand on clutter.

`tests/podhue.test.ts` checks the hues (stable, never shared, far from every state colour, the same as the tokens) and `tests/pod-label.test.ts` the labels' words, colours and rolls and where the zones and labels lie. `node design/shoot-pods.mjs` takes the shots.

### The sightline rules

Nothing taller than 1.1 m stands between the mission table and the consoles, and the south wall is only a 0.4 m curb, so a person at the table (or the Overview) sees every unit. `tests/layout.test.ts` checks it, along with every seat id, that every seat can be walked to from the lift, and that the ready line has a walkable tick for each unit that needs someone.

From the captain's seated eye, nothing stands in front of the situation arc: every console's hood, a person standing anywhere on either tier, the holo at its tallest (2.3 m) and the board agents stay at least 8 cm under the line to the arc's foot, and the arc hangs at least 0.6 m under the ceiling (`tests/sightline.test.ts`). The same test holds the chair's framing to its bands: at 1440x900 the arc takes over a fifth of the frame's height and two fifths of its width, a quarter of bow over it, the holo table and four or more of the crew under it.

### Walking the tiers

There are no stairs to climb: walking reads the floor's height from `heightAt` (`src/client/player/collide.ts`). A rise of up to 0.5 m is a step (a riser, a tier's ramped end, the aisle and the galleries, which rise smoothly under their drawn treads), the camera gliding up or down it; anything taller is a wall, so the dais's back, the galleries' outer edges and the back tier's back edge hold you rather than drop you (with brass rails along them). The routes the office plans (`src/shared/nav.ts`) keep off those ledges too. `tests/amphitheater.test.ts` walks the captain from the lift onto the dais, down the aisle into the pit and back, and up each gallery, and `design/walk-check.mjs` does it in the real office with the keys.

### The forward lounge

Behind the situation arc, in the bow bay between the hull frames at x 0 and 6.63 (`src/shared/lounge.ts`, built by `src/client/features/lounge/world.ts`), a balcony hangs from the canopy on three rods: deck plate laid with the floor's grid, a graphite fascia with the tiers' lit ship-cyan lip along its nosing, a brushed steel guard rail on posts round its open sides, a low ledge along the glass and warm downlights in its underside. Its deck is level with the bow glass's sill, so up there the glass runs from your feet to over your head; the light-strip channel that crosses the bow glass elsewhere stops at the frames either side of the bay. Three lounge seats (`view-1` to `view-3`) stand along the glass, fanned a little to the bow, and seated your eye is about 3.3 m over the deck.

- **Getting up there.** A steel ladder stands on the deck against the balcony's south face, its stringers carried on as grab posts that curve back over onto it, and a spring gate between them. E at its foot climbs it (`features/lounge/climb.ts`, after upstream agent-office's ladder): you square up to it in 0.4 s, go up hand over hand at about 1.7 m/s, each gloved hand planted on its rung while you rise past it and going over the other to the next with the rung's clank as it lands (`features/lounge/grips.ts`), your eyes following your hands, and step over its head onto the balcony with a little hop while the gate swings open and shuts behind you. E at the gate takes you back down: turned round onto the rungs, down, and off the foot facing the room. W or S turns a climb round on the rungs. With less motion it climbs at an even pace with no hop, and the head turns at once.
- **Sitting there.** E at a seat sits you facing the bow; in first person the view widens by 9 degrees and lifts 0.2 rad over the horizon, so the stars, the destination, a planet or a flyby and the jumps fill the frame, and you can look anywhere. A slim readout on the glass in front of the seats (`features/lounge/readout.ts`) names a unit that needs you or is stuck, in its hue, with N to go straight to it, and with nobody calling counts a waypoint's jump in; only the urgent units keep their arrows at the edge of the view while you sit there. The hint steps back to *Esc Stand up* after 3 s; Esc, E or a step gets you up behind the seat.
- **Walking round it.** Under it there is 1.98 m of headroom: you walk beneath it to the glass, and a jump there bumps your head. Up on it the rail keeps you in, higher than a jump (`loungeColliders`); the gap at the ladder's head is closed by its gate, so you get down by climbing. The units' routes keep off its footprint and the ladder's foot.
- **From the chair.** Everything of it that stands on the balcony is behind the arc's boards from the captain's eye, and its fascia sits under the arc's foot (`tests/lounge.test.ts`). None of it casts a shadow, so the deck's merge folds it into a few draws.

## Units

A unit is one agent at its console: a faceless figure about 1.3 m tall on a hover base, with a flat head plate, a dark visor strip that flickers as its terminal prints, and a band round its chest. Nothing about it is decoration: its state shows four ways at once, all read from the building's one ranking (`src/shared/attention.ts`), so it agrees with the top bar, the Units rail, the Attention board and Mission control.

| State | Band | Ring on the floor | Glyph over it | Where it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Needs you | Signal orange | orange, a pulse spreading from it every 1.2 s | solid diamond | on its pod's ready line, facing the table, with a faint orange shaft over it |
| Stuck | red, blinking at 0.5 Hz | red, with a hatched band inside it | hollow triangle with a bar | at its console, darker and slumped forward |
| To review | amber | amber outline | hollow circle with a dot | at its console, turned toward the Review bay |
| Working | steel, breathing | faint steel | none | at its console |
| Parked | dark | none | none | at its console or on the Standby bench; dimmer still when its process is asleep |
| Merged | violet, for 6 s once its pull request merges | violet | check in a square | at its console |

Over each unit is a callout with its **call sign**, the seat it holds: `A-03` is the third console of pod A, `O-` the overflow bay, `S-` the Standby bench, `L-` a board agent's lectern and `R-` a chair in the Review bay (`src/shared/callsign.ts`, which the Units list and the Attention board use too). How much a callout says depends on how close you are (`src/client/features/workers/lod.ts`). From far off (the Overview zoomed out past a half-height of 13 m, or a unit more than 14 m away on foot) it is a small square tab in the state's hue with the glyph and no words; one that needs you or is stuck keeps its call sign beside the tab, and the selected unit (see [Selecting a unit](controls.md#selecting-a-unit)) shows one line at any distance, never its tab nor its card, since the selection card has the rest. In the middle distance it is one mono line: the glyph, the call sign and what the unit is doing now, its latest tool call cut to 22 characters (`Bash: npm test`, `Edit worker.ts`), or the ranking's label when it is done or stuck (`Done: PR ready`). Up close (a half-height under 6 m, or under 5 m on foot) it is a three-line card: the call sign and name muted with a status chip at the right (the state's word in its hue, the same word the rail and the card lead with, and the deck's one clock, ticking each second: `<1m` under a minute), the task in bold (its one-line summary where that says more than the short name, else the whole task, on up to two lines, cut with `...` only where it runs over, never a short name that looks complete), and its branch, PR and engine muted under it, the engine named as the selection card names it (a stuck unit's reason in its hue there instead; a unit that needs you says what it asks, or `Needs an answer` in its hue when the question has no words of its own). A `[ask]` or `[perm]` tag never shows on a callout. Walking with a unit selected, a neighbour within 300 px of it on screen steps back to its tab (with its call sign when it needs someone), so its card never sits over the selected one's, and the needs-you diamond or stuck triangle over a unit stands down while its card shows (the chip says it). Each boundary has a 10% band, so a callout never flickers between two tiers at the edge. When the tier changes, each unit waits a delay of its own and its callout pops in from 88% size and clear, rising 6 px into place, over 220 ms, so a zoom ripples across the crew: the units that need someone at once, then the rest one at a time out from the middle of the view, 45 ms apart and never later than 360 ms. On the move up into the Overview or back down (G) every callout is a tab and fades out over about the move's first quarter, then stays out of sight until the view has landed, when they ripple in that way, fading up over 90 ms. A unit that comes on the deck pops in the same way, and with reduced motion it is a cut. Callouts never cover each other (`src/client/features/workers/declutter.ts`): they are placed in the ranking's order, one in the way lifts a little, then shrinks to its call sign, and a working unit's with no room at all hides, while one that needs someone always shows. From the Overview the pods' ground labels and the bodies of the units that must show are there first, and every callout keeps clear of them; a must-show callout lifted more than half its height or slid aside gets a hairline in its state's hue back to its unit. A unit whose callout is hidden shows its glyph alone, the same size on screen however far off it is. The provider's letter is on the visor (C Claude Code, X Codex, P Pi, CU Cursor) and a muted stripe in the provider's tint runs down its back.

The ready line keeps its order: a unit on it keeps its tick when the ranking moves, a new one takes the lowest free tick on its pod, a fifth one on a pod starts a second row, and a unit that has been answered holds its tick for 8 s before it glides back, so a state that flips back and forth doesn't send it to and fro (its ring changes color at once). Four lights hang over the four units most in need on the deck, orange or red: never one light per unit. `src/client/features/readyline/` does both, and `tests/units.test.ts` checks the ticks and the call signs.

A new unit builds up from its hover base in half a second, and one stood down goes dark, lifts off its pad and folds away into a line of light. Units glide, leaning a couple of degrees into the move; nothing hops, walks or bobs. Under reduced motion, glides and the build-up are cuts, the pulse is a still ring and the stuck blink is a steady band.

The people on the deck are **operators**: the same family at 1.75 m, with a narrow head plate and a visor slit, a light steel shell (so a person never reads as a unit) and a shoulder yoke in their own color, the one place it shows. While they talk the yoke's edge brightens and a mic tick lights beside their mono name plate. Settings > Your operator picks the shell's tone, the head plate and its tone, and the yoke.

### Heartbeat ring and quiet meter

Two marks on the floor say whether a working unit is really working, from `src/client/features/heartbeat/`:

- **The heartbeat ring.** Each real tool call (a hook event) swells a thin line of a ring off the unit's quiet meter, 0.8 m to 1.12 m, 1.4 times out, over 600 ms (short of the next seat), coming up over its first 80 ms and fading as it goes. Its hue is what the call was: ship-cyan reading, steel-white editing, green a test or build, mauve the web, stuck red a check failing. A unit pulses at most once every 1.2 s, however fast it calls, and arriving on a deck never sets off a burst: only a call made after you got there pulses.
- **The quiet meter.** A thin 300-degree arc at 0.75 m under each working unit, at half strength, its gap toward the console; the dark disc a unit's ring otherwise sits on is left out under a working unit, so the meter, its pulse and its status ring don't stack up. It is full right after any sign of life (a hook event, terminal output, starting work: the same moment the ranking counts from, `lastSign` in `src/shared/attention.ts`) and drains clockwise over `TOOL_SILENT_MS`, the soonest the ranking can call a quiet unit stuck: ship-cyan while under half drained, amber from half, blending from one to the other between 45% and 55% rather than switching in a frame; a tick at halfway marks where it turns. Drained, it stays an empty amber track while the unit is still ranked working; once the ranking calls it stuck, its stuck marks take over and the meter goes.

Only a unit shown as working has a meter: one that needs you, is stuck or waits for review keeps its own marks, so nothing says two things at once. While any unit needs you the rings drop to 30% and the meters to half, as everything on the deck yields to needs-you. With Ship motion Off, or reduced motion, the rings stop and the meters still show. Both marks are one instanced draw each for the whole deck, and a frame allocates nothing (`design/heartbeat-bench.ts` times it with 24 units); `design/shoot-heartbeat.mjs` shoots a busy unit beside a silent one, and `tests/heartbeat.test.ts` checks the numbers.

## Walk and Overview

You arrive in the view you were last in: **Walk** (first person; third person is in Settings) the first time, or the **Overview** if you left from there, in which case the arrival shot ends by rising from the conn into it (`src/client/features/homeview/`, remembered in this browser as `ao.homeView`). Sitting in the captain's chair frames the bridge (`src/client/features/amphitheater/`): the field of view closes from 55 to 50 degrees and the view is aimed 30% of the way up the situation arc, so the bow and the arc fill the top half of the frame and the pit and the tiers with the crew the bottom half. You can still look anywhere, and the focus lean takes over once you move the mouse. On a page's first load the view comes in from outside: a 5 s arrival shot from ahead of the bow, past the destination world and down through the canopy to the conn; any key, click or mouse move lands you there at once, and it doesn't play with less motion or with a unit already waiting (see [the cinema](design.md#the-cinema)). With the Units rail open, the seated view is centred on the canvas right of it (`src/client/features/seatframe/`), so the arc's port wing is never under the rail. Press **G** for the **Overview**: the whole deck from above at 48 degrees, with no perspective, the shot to show someone the deck in one look. The view rises into it from your eyes in 650 ms, under way on the first frame and settling long, its field of view closing onto the Overview's framing, and the orthographic camera takes over on the frame where the two draw the same picture; G or Esc dollies back down into your eyes the same way (`src/client/core/overview-transition.ts`). Keys and the mouse wait for the move to land, and the key legend fades in once it has. Left alone for 8 s, the Overview drifts by half a degree and 15 cm on slow sines that never line up, so the shot stays alive; any input stops it where it is. Each trip up starts 30 degrees off the axis on the starboard side, so the deck reads as a plan: the dais and the tiers near, the pit and the holo in the middle, the arc beyond with its boards turned toward you, fitted to the whole frame between the top bar and the bottom bar. From up there the hull and the floor are taken toward a dark slate (never a state's mark) and the light shafts go (from above even a trace read as smudges on the floor), so the units, the pods and their states stand out. The deck's polished surfaces go matte over the first stretch of the move up and the exposure rises by a quarter with it (`src/client/features/atmos/gloss.ts`): the plan still reads, and the camera never sweeps through the key light's reflection off the table top on the way: no frame of the move is more than 10 brighter or darker than the one before (`design/shoot-overview.mjs` checks it on the GPU). With Mission control docked, the framing keeps clear of it.

| --- | --- |
| Q / E | Turn the deck a quarter (280 ms) |
| W A S D / arrows, or drag | Pan |
| Wheel | Zoom |
| Click a unit / bare deck | Select it, or let go (see [Selecting a unit](controls.md#selecting-a-unit)) |

A window opened from the Overview closes back to it with no extra click; one opened from Walk closes straight back to mouse-look. Going to a unit (N, a click on its toast, a search result) flies the view there in a 700 ms arc in Walk (`src/client/core/flight.ts`), or pans and zooms the Overview onto it in 900 ms, most of the way at once and then a long settle (ease-out quint), closer if it was further out but never further out than it was, landing it in the middle of the deck you can see, right of the Units rail and left of a docked Mission control (`flyTo` in `src/client/core/camera-overview.ts`). The wheel's zoom eases (about 70 ms) into the point under the pointer, and takes over from a flight under way. Under reduced motion or Ship motion Off, the move up and down, the turn and every flight are cuts, and there is no drift; the demo's orbit owns the turn, so it doesn't drift either. In demo mode (`?demo=1`) the Overview sways slowly round the table, fitted so every pod plate stays in frame, until you take over; see [docs/design.md](design.md#demo-mode).

Every clock that says how long a unit has waited on someone takes its tone from one rule (`waitTone` in `src/shared/wait.ts`): fresh (muted) under 5 minutes, bold from 5, heavier and underlined from 30. A clock escalates by weight, never by colour: the colours already say what a unit is, so a late clock never borrows the yellow of to review or the red of stuck. The rail, Mission control, the selection card, the callouts over the units, the pod plates, the Attention board, the needs-you chip and the bottom bar's N chip all use it; a working unit's time never takes a tone. The clocks tick to the minute with a short fade and ease their colour over 200 ms when the tone changes, both cut under reduced motion (`src/client/ui/waitclock.ts`). From the Overview a unit that needs you or is stuck keeps its call sign, a short ask and its wait in its callout ("A-03 npm publish? 12m", a permission leading with the command), each pod plate's lead count carries its oldest wait ("1 needs you 12m"), and the N chip says who the next press of N goes to ("next: Byte, 12m"), moving on with each press and staying in the bottom bar from the Overview. The first in line keeps the whole of what it asks in its callout ("Update the snapshot or fix the selector?"), except on a phone. A callout that needs someone draws over a pod plate rather than leaving its unit, and the plate dims under it; where two callouts still meet, the more urgent one is on top. A pod plate never shrinks its counts to fit: the calm ones at the end give way to "+N". When one of a pod's units is answered or its pull request merges, its plate's rim glows in the merged hue: full for 120 ms, then easing out by 900 ms (never on a snooze or a unit standing down, and not under reduced motion). With Mission control docked, G, N and Tab still work on the deck; the panel takes only the keys it uses itself.

## The merge beat

A merge is the one moment the deck celebrates, and it does it with light, not confetti. When a person merges a unit's pull request, a violet pulse runs from the unit's console to the mission table and its rim lights. When the bounty for it is released on devnet, the pulse runs on across the floor to the Proof corner and up the attestation rail, and parks as the rail's new lit segment; the vault's lid lifts with a violet glow, the top bar's violet counter rolls, and a proof toast shows the devnet transaction in mono with a settled tick and an explorer link. Then the bounty's coins fly out of the vault over the deck to the console of the unit that earned it, and a receipt floats up over the console for six seconds: the amount, the unit and its cell, the pull request and the devnet transaction. You hear them go: a tink as each leaves the vault, their rush across the deck, a tink as each lands and a soft chord ([Sound](design.md#sound)). An attestation landing on Base Sepolia plays the merged cue and a toast of its own, and a unit's first ERC-8004 record makes the plinth glow as its step lights. A unit deployed to a console gets a shorter beat of its own: a steel trace from the table out to it. The camera never moves for a beat, and under reduced motion there is no traveling light: the rail and the lid change at once and the toast says the rest (`src/client/features/beats/`).

## The bridge

The room is the bridge of a starship, built round the same plan (`src/client/features/bridge/`, fixtures on the floor's plan like every other):

- **Viewports.** The bow is one band of glass 26 m wide along the north wall, 2.2 to 6.5 m up, behind and over the situation arc, the hull's three frames standing across it as heavy ribs; from the captain's chair the arc covers its foot and space shows over the arc through the canopy. Down the east and west walls each bay has a wide, low port at a seated eye's height with its ends rounded off, and a slim strip over it, clear of the Review bay, the violet rail and the docs rack. Every port has a bezel with a dim cyan line round the glass inside and a bright one outside, no mullions, and glass that thickens to a cold tint at a slant. Over the south curb is the aft glass. The openings are `WINDOWS` in `src/shared/layout.ts`; `tests/layout.test.ts` keeps them clear of what hangs on the walls.
- **The hull.** A frame up the walls under every rib of the canopy, each with a ship-cyan line down its face, a ship-cyan cove along the foot of the walls and another where the walls meet the canopy, washing down the top of the wall, and seams at the viewports' sills and heads.
- **The canopy.** Sixteen ribs from a halo ring 9 m over the table out to the tops of the walls, two rings of purlins, and dark glass between them.
- **Outside.** The ship the bridge is part of: plated walls, a sloped skirt from their feet to the hull's edge rising at the bow into a glacis, an armoured brow along their tops, a chamfered hull under the slab with its bow to the north, a lit edge round it, two nacelles aft with their drive glow and hot cores, and running lights (`features/bridge/skin.ts`): red to port and green to starboard for the Overview only, white strobes at the bow and nacelles.
- **Space.** Round the ship (`src/client/features/space/`): a sky with the galactic band and its dust lanes crossing the forward viewport on a slant, a teal and indigo nebula just right of the bow with a lobe of it out of each side's ports, its filaments, knots and dark lanes, and crisp stars, baked once into a cube at load; three layers of stars streaming aft past the glass as the ship makes way north; the galactic band tilted to cross the side ports too; a meteor every 20 to 40 s; and every few minutes a planet or moon across a side port (clouds, a lit limb, a ring round a gas giant), an asteroid field tumbling past, or a comet across the forward glass. The ship makes way faster with each merge in the last hour (0.4x to 1.6x) and holds station when no unit is deployed or all are parked, and the drive glow follows. A merge surges it: the stars streak for 1.4 s and the glass glints. A milestone done jumps it: the stars stretch toward the bow, the view widens a few degrees, a glint comes up over the glass (a third by Night), and the ship comes out in a new region of space. Ship motion at Off removes all streaming past the ports; Calm keeps the stars but drops the streaks. The sky is only ever seen through glass, so none of it sits behind a board's text, and it keeps to neutrals, blues, teals and ship-cyan, never a state's hue. Close by (`src/client/features/vista/`): sheets of gas and dust streaming past the side ports at four depths, a ringed gas giant out of the port side about a third of a port across, and the sun high over the bow with its flare, cut by the canopy's ribs as you move under them (see [the design system](design.md#space-outside-the-glass)).
- **The world outside** (`src/client/features/destination/`, `fleet/`, `sorties/`). Dead ahead in the canopy, the mission's destination: a bright point at the start that grows with real progress only (waypoints passed and the open one's issues closed) to a third of the forward view, with a mono band under it ("MAKING FOR AUTH REWRITE - WAYPOINT 2 OF 4 - 35%", and "BEHIND SCHEDULE: 4 DAYS" while the waypoint is overdue), markers astern for the waypoints passed, and orbit once every one is passed. Off the side ports, every other deck as an escort in a V, its class by its units, its ports lit per unit at work, its name on its flank, a needs-you diamond over its bridge while a unit there waits on someone (click the ship for the Decks lift). Round the hull, a fighter for each unit at work patrolling past its pod's side port; open pull requests hold on the picket ahead of the bow; a merged one's fighter runs home to the hangar as the merge beat fires; a unit that needs you or is stuck has its fighter drift dark outside the glass. High off the starboard bow, over the arc's starboard wing from the chair, stands the Relay Beacon (`src/client/features/relay/`), seen from the captain's chair: a lattice tower with a warm lamp at the heart of three turning rings, a node-star on them for each unit at work in the fleet, the lamp flaring on a merge and a ledger ring lighting a segment per bounty released. See [docs/design.md](design.md#the-world-outside) and [the Relay Beacon](design.md#the-relay-beacon).
- **The crew** (`src/client/features/vesper/`, `crew/`, `droid/`, `mascot/`). VESPER's lines ride at the head of the ticker's log under its name. Units wear their epithet on their callout up close and thin chevrons on the outside of the left shoulder. On the Proof corner's top step the unit of the watch stands as a still hologram in a cone of cool white light, a plaque over it with its call sign, epithet and record on the last watch. Bolt, the bridge droid, docks on a charger on the west wall south of the Proof corner, flies at walking pace over the units' heads, round the table and in through the Review bay's door. Kip, the bridge mascot (`features/mascot/`), naps in a parts tray on the deck under that charger and runs on foot: the pit lane 0.9 m out from the table's rim, out past the west end of the tiers to his nest, through the Review bay's door, and up the centre aisle to the dais, the only way up for someone 0.6 m tall. None of it is in the Overview. See [docs/design.md](design.md#the-crew).
- **Rituals** (`src/client/features/launch/`, `drive/`, `turnaround/`, where they stand in `src/shared/ritual-slots.ts`). The drive core rises out of the Deck lift's roof, framed by the aft glass: twelve rings round a shader core, a ring lit ship-cyan for each merge in the current run and today's best etched on it as a thin white line, its light running up the column at the ship's cruise speed. The fleet's eight-week tally hangs over the Services panel. The pit wall, the captain's turnaround clock, stands on a mast over the Review bay, high enough to clear the Issues panel from the conn; when a wait clears fast a hairline runs from the bay's door down the west aisle and along the aft of the deck to the lift. The start of watch's log is typed onto a flat panel high over the forward view, as if projected on the glass (a page panel, so no rib cuts it). None of it is in the Overview but the hairline. See [docs/design.md](design.md#rituals).
- **Forward displays.** Each board of the situation arc, and the capacity strip, sits in a graphite bezel with a ship-cyan hairline over and under it. The counts are the Attention board's own band, so nothing hangs over the arc to repeat them; the ticker runs along its top. Every board is laid out the same way (`src/client/features/boards/screen.ts`): its name big and white in its own title bar with its count at the right, then as many rows as the board's height takes (two on a wing's board, three on the Attention board, four at most), the most in need first, each with its state's stripe and glyph, a first line in 0.24 m type that reads from the conn (0.28 m on the Attention board, whose names are short) and a smaller second line for up close, cut with an ellipsis rather than run off, and "+N more" under them. Nothing else on the deck is drawn over a board's text: the holo's stars and its cone fade out where a board's face is on screen, and a unit's callout that would cover a board docks in a slot row under that board's lower bezel, still tied to its unit by its hairline (see [the design system](design.md#the-bridge-from-the-captains-chair)). The canvases have the panels' own proportions, are painted at 300 pixels a metre, and are filtered with mipmaps and the renderer's most anisotropy (`src/client/world/sharp.ts`), so a board seen at a slant stays sharp. The capacity strip is one line: its name and whether there's room, CPU and memory each as a percent on a bar, and the units against the limit.
- **Stations.** Each console has a hull fin at either end and a ship-cyan trace along the edge of its top where its unit's hands rest. The face of its pedestal, toward the table, is a station screen: its unit's state glyph in its own hue and shape on the left (a dash at work or standing by), and on the right a readout. A working station's readout runs ship-cyan bars that scroll and grow with its terminal's output, a faint scan line passes every 6 s, and three blinkers twinkle at its end on slow periods of their own (4.3, 6.1 and 8.9 s). A stuck station shows still red hatching, one to review a steady amber line, one standing by a dim dash and one steady blinker, an empty console nothing.
- **Life** (`src/client/features/life/`). A working unit wears ship-cyan whatever its output (its band, its visor and a soft halo on the floor that breathes faster the busier it is); its hands stay at the console and work it harder while its terminal prints, its head bobs a little, and motes of cyan rise off its station's screen, more the busier it is. Now and then a busy station sends a data pulse, a point of ship-cyan light in a low arc, from its hood to the holo table, where dashes run along the course to the ship. Round the plot a band of light lettering turns slowly and says where the ship is making for and how far it has come ("CAPTAIN, WE ARE 40% OF THE WAY TO AUTH REWRITE", issues closed of those linked to the active milestone, never a made-up figure), the waypoint, the issues still out and the units on it; a progress ring on the tabletop shows the same fraction. Over the Attention board a ticker hangs along the arc's top: the ship's clock ticking by the second and how long the deck has been under way (since its earliest working unit started, or "holding station"), then the deck's log, the timeline's latest events, running right to left.
- **The amphitheatre** (`src/client/features/amphitheater/`). A ship-cyan ring round the pit's edge. The tiers' tops are laid with the deck's own floor, their risers in console graphite, each with a lit ship-cyan lip along its nosing and a toe light along its foot; the aisle is a flight of eight solid steps from the pit to the dais's lip, a lit nosing on each; brass rails run up the aisle's high end, along the galleries' outer edges and the back tier's back. Built from the same heights walking reads, merged by material: a handful of draws.

The canopy, the aft glass, the ticker and the condition band over the arc are on a layer of their own that the walk camera sees and the Overview's doesn't, so the Overview still looks straight down into the room; nothing on that layer can be clicked. The holo plot turns slowly round the table (half a turn a minute) and stands still under reduced motion. The bridge's life gives way to attention: for 3 s after a unit starts needing you or gets stuck everything ambient dims to 40%, a pod with such a unit stays hushed (its stations' bars at 30%, no pulses, no blinkers) while it lasts, and the whole bridge runs a quarter quieter while anyone is waiting on you. Calm halves it; Off, or reduced motion, stills it, and the screens still read. Settings > Deck > Life sets how much the bridge lives: Full, Calm (no salutes, hail lines or patrols) or Silent running (no ambient life, the stars at a crawl, the ticker paused), with a switch each for the destination, the fleet and the squadron. Settings > Deck > Ship motion sets how space moves: Full, Calm (half speed, no flybys, meteors or streaks) or Off (still, with the rest of the deck, as reduced motion is; a waypoint then crossfades the view in 400 ms).

Ship-cyan (`#6FC3DF`, `#2C5E70` for anything that glows over an area) is the instruments' own color and the working units' accent, never an attention state: hairlines, small type, the holo and the units at work. A state's hue only ever shows on a unit, its ring and glyph, its callout, its station's screen, the ready line and the counts.

## Light and materials

The bridge has two lights, picked in Settings > Deck > Bridge lights: **Night** (low light, the default while the system is dark) and **Day** (high light), or **Auto**, which follows the system's dark or light setting. The rig is one table, `LIGHT_MODES` in `src/client/features/lights/modes.ts`: a cool fill from the sky and the slate below, a key through the forward viewport from high over the bow that throws the only shadows, a fill from aft through the aft glass, two low rims from east and west that cut units and consoles out of the floor, a spot over each pod, one over the table and a ship-cyan uplight in the holo table (`features/lights/rig.ts`). A mode only retunes those lights and never adds or takes one away, since that would recompile every material. By night the key is a cool blue-indigo starlight from over the bow with rims low from either side, the fill from the sky is low so the hull stays dark, the pods' lamps are a warm white, and a glow (bloom, threshold 0.8) lifts what is brightest. By day the ship is in high orbit over a sunlit planet: a warm, hard sun key, a cool fill, and the hull, walls and floor a mid-dark plate (albedo about 0.3 at most: the sun lifts them rather than whitening them), the consoles go darker still into graphite blocks, units stay graphite, lettering on the walls and floor turns dark, and there is no glow. Brightness steps every light up or down by 12% a step, two steps either way; the boards, callouts and state marks give their own light, so they read the same at every step. Each unit's marks sit on a 6 cm disc of instrument black (`#101720`) and every callout on a 92% dark chip, so a state's hue reads at 4.5:1 or better in both modes (`tests/lights.test.ts`). The room never tints with a state, by day or by night. Each surface has one of four finishes (`FINISH` in `src/client/world/office/materials.ts`): brushed steel and rails at roughness 0.35, the consoles' glass tops at 0.1, wall and deck plate at 0.6, rubber at 0.85; the units keep their own two-tone shells with a rim in their state's colour. Every matte surface carries the trim atlas (`src/client/world/office/trim.ts`), one 1024 texture baked at start with the plating's seams, bolt rows, vents, dirt in the seams and wear along their edges, cast from the world's own axes, so it needs no uvs and no draws. The consoles, hoods, the Standby bench and the lecterns have rounded edges, and cable trays, conduits, clamps and ribs with lightening holes run along the east, west and north walls (`greebles.ts`, four instanced draws), with lit channels in the starlight's indigo along the walls and up each rib (two more). The floor's tiles each take a shade of their own, and its walkways (round the table outside the ready lines, and out along the aisles) are scuffed and polished, reflecting the room (`floor.ts`). There are soft contact shadows under what stands on the floor, and lit hairlines along the edges that have to read in a dark frame: the slab, the walls' tops, the table's rim, the lift's portal, the hull's frames and the viewports.

The air and the floor carry the light too (`src/client/features/atmos/`). Shafts of light, warm with the sun, hang under five of the canopy's panes (falling the way the key's shadows do) and in at each low side port, never over a station, with dust drifting only inside them; a haze lies low over the floor, under about a metre, tinted by the sky ahead; pools of light lie on the floor under the boards, round the holo table, on the table side of each console, along the cove and inside the side ports; and the table's spot throws the canopy's ribs across the table and the deck round it. Light from outside reaches the room through the rig it already has: the key and the fill take the hue of the sky ahead and abeam, a passing planet's colour comes in through its port on the rim from that side, a comet or a meteor drags a glint across everything glossy, and the jump's flash washes the floor and the units ship-cyan. At High the pit's polished floor and the centre aisle mirror the wall boards. None of it touches an attention mark (they are `fog: false` and carry their own light), and when a unit needs the captain or is stuck the shafts, the dust, the ribs and the planet's wash duck to 60% for 2.5 s and then stand at 85% while anyone waits; a dark ring round the waiting unit on screen picks it out ([the look](design.md#the-look-value-light-and-colour)).

What the surfaces reflect is image-based light (`src/client/features/ibl/`): a probe over the table sees only what gives light inside (the practicals, the boards, the strips, the holo) and is the environment of everything inside, and a second one sees only the sky and lights the hull's outside. The room's probe is captured once the floor arrives and again on a change of the lights, Brightness or the alert condition, a face a frame; it never holds the sky, so nothing on the deck reflects the moving stars. The droid's charger is a cradle on the sill of a west port, under the glass rather than in front of it.

Settings > Deck > Quality sets how much the deck draws: **Auto** (the default), **Low**, **Medium** or **High** (`src/client/features/quality/`). Auto starts from the graphics the browser names: Apple silicon and discrete GPUs draw at High, Intel and other integrated ones at Medium, software renderers at Low. Once the deck has loaded it watches the frames, leaving out the few seconds after anything that hitches once (a Night and Day switch, a jump, a floor arriving, the tab coming back, a burst of terminal output). Frames that keep falling behind for ten seconds step it down one tier, never twice in a minute; half a minute with room to spare steps it back up. On Apple silicon and discrete GPUs it holds at Medium or better unless frames are very slow. A step down is kept for a reload in the same browser session, never longer than a day, so a slow minute never holds a fast machine at Low. Under the row a chip says what it draws at now, *Auto - running at High*, with its last step and why (*stepped to Medium at 14:02, slow frames*); the menu (Tab) has it on its Quality row under Deck (*Auto - Medium since 14:02, slow frames*), with *Try High* to forget a step down at once. A tier picked by hand stays put. High draws 1.5 pixels per CSS pixel, the full glow, a 2048 shadow map every frame, every shaft of light with 1,500 motes in them and the floor's mirror of the boards; Medium 1.25 pixels, the glow at half size, a 1024 shadow map 20 times a second and the canopy's shafts with 800 motes; Low one pixel, no glow, a 1024 shadow map drawn again only when someone moves, two of the three star layers, no room light, sky light or trim (a software renderer feels every lookup), and of the light round the deck only the haze and the pools. The canopy's ribs and the light from outside are drawn at Medium and High. Every tier draws the boards, the marks and the callouts the same; Medium and Low leave a unit's smallest parts (its stripe, chest mark and letters, its laptop's hairline) out from further off. Switching applies at once, without recompiling anything. The viewport glass is almost clear looking straight through and thickens to a cold tint at a slant. The units' shells catch a cool rim light at their edges, so they hold their silhouette against the dark consoles. There are no outlines, no plants and no model files; everything is built in code. The colors are DESIGN.md's 3D row.
