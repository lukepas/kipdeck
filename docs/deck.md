# The deck

Back to the [README](../README.md).

Every project is a deck, and the room on it is the bridge of a ship under way: one operations floor inside a hull, the same on every project, built as a command amphitheatre. The mission table stands in a pit at deck level; south of it the deck steps up in two curved tiers of consoles to the captain's raised dais; the situation arc hangs over the north side, in front of the bow. It is built so that a glance from the captain's chair (or from the Overview above it) tells you which units need a person, which are stuck and which are only at work. The plan is `src/shared/layout.ts`, which the server checks seats against and the 2D view reads too, so the 3D deck and the rest never drift apart.

## What's where

The deck is a 32 m square. North is the bow and the situation arc's side; the Deck lift stands in the middle of the south curb, its portal facing north, with the dais just north of it. You arrive on the dais at the captain's right hand, looking down over the tiers and the pit to the Attention board and the bow. The floor's height anywhere is `heightAt(x, z)` in `src/shared/amphitheater.ts` (re-exported by `layout.ts`), which walking, the units, the light and the tests all read.

### Cell addresses

A grid of columns every 4 m is stencilled round the deck's edge: letters A to H west to east, numbers 1 to 8 north to south. Every spot is in a cell, like `C4`, and the Attention board names each unit's cell. `cellOf(x, z)` in `src/shared/layout.ts` works one out.

### Pods and goals

A pod takes on the goal most of the units at its consoles work toward. The task queue seats a new unit with a goal in that goal's pod while it has a free console, then in a pod nobody has claimed yet, then wherever is free (`src/shared/pods.ts`). A unit whose goal changes keeps its console.

### The sightline rules

Nothing taller than 1.1 m stands between the mission table and the consoles, and the south wall is only a 0.4 m curb, so a person at the table (or the Overview) sees every unit. `tests/layout.test.ts` checks it, along with every seat id, that every seat can be walked to from the lift, and that the ready line has a walkable tick for each unit that needs someone.

From the captain's seated eye, nothing stands in front of the situation arc: every console's hood, a person standing anywhere on either tier, the holo at its tallest (2.3 m) and the board agents stay at least 8 cm under the line to the arc's foot, and the arc hangs at least 0.6 m under the ceiling (`tests/sightline.test.ts`). The same test holds the chair's framing to its bands: at 1440x900 the arc takes over a fifth of the frame's height and two fifths of its width, a quarter of bow over it, the holo table and four or more of the crew under it.

### Walking the tiers

There are no stairs to climb: walking reads the floor's height from `heightAt` (`src/client/player/collide.ts`). A rise of up to 0.5 m is a step (a riser, a tier's ramped end, the aisle and the galleries, which rise smoothly under their drawn treads), the camera gliding up or down it; anything taller is a wall, so the dais's back, the galleries' outer edges and the back tier's back edge hold you rather than drop you (with brass rails along them). The routes the office plans (`src/shared/nav.ts`) keep off those ledges too. `tests/amphitheater.test.ts` walks the captain from the lift onto the dais, down the aisle into the pit and back, and up each gallery, and `design/walk-check.mjs` does it in the real office with the keys.

### The forward lounge

Behind the situation arc, in the bow bay between the hull frames at x 0 and 6.63 (`src/shared/lounge.ts`, built by `src/client/features/lounge/world.ts`), a balcony hangs from the canopy on three rods: deck plate laid with the floor's grid, a graphite fascia with the tiers' lit ship-cyan lip along its nosing, a brushed steel guard rail on posts round its open sides, a low ledge along the glass and warm downlights in its underside. Its deck is level with the bow glass's sill, so up there the glass runs from your feet to over your head; the light-strip channel that crosses the bow glass elsewhere stops at the frames either side of the bay. Three lounge seats (`view-1` to `view-3`) stand along the glass, fanned a little to the bow, and seated your eye is about 3.3 m over the deck.

- **Getting up there.** A steel ladder stands on the deck against the balcony's south face, its stringers carried on as grab posts that curve back over onto it, and a spring gate between them. E at its foot climbs it (`features/lounge/climb.ts`, after upstream agent-office's ladder): you square up to it in 0.4 s, go up hand over hand at about 1.7 m/s, each gloved hand planted on its rung while you rise past it and going over the other to the next with the rung's clank as it lands (`features/lounge/grips.ts`), your eyes following your hands, and step over its head onto the balcony with a little hop while the gate swings open and shuts behind you. E at the gate takes you back down: turned round onto the rungs, down, and off the foot facing the room. W or S turns a climb round on the rungs. With less motion it climbs at an even pace with no hop, and the head turns at once.
- **Sitting there.** E at a seat sits you facing the bow; in first person the view widens by 9 degrees and lifts 0.2 rad over the horizon, so the stars, the destination, a planet or a flyby and the jumps fill the frame, and you can look anywhere. A slim readout on the glass in front of the seats (`features/lounge/readout.ts`) names a unit that needs you or is stuck, in its hue, with N to go straight to it, and with nobody calling counts a waypoint's jump in; only the urgent units keep their arrows at the edge of the view while you sit there. The hint steps back to *Esc Stand up* after 3 s; Esc, E or a step gets you up behind the seat.
- **Walking round it.** Under it there is 1.98 m of headroom: you walk beneath it to the glass, and a jump there bumps your head. Up on it the rail keeps you in, higher than a jump (`loungeColliders`); the gap at the ladder's head is closed by its gate, so you get down by climbing. The units' routes keep off its footprint and the ladder's foot.
- **From the chair.** Everything of it that stands on the balcony is behind the arc's boards from the captain's eye, and its fascia sits under the arc's foot (`tests/lounge.test.ts`). None of it casts a shadow, so the deck's merge folds it into a few draws.

## Units

A unit is one agent at its console: a faceless figure about 1.3 m tall on a hover base, with a flat head plate, a dark visor strip that flickers as its terminal prints, and a band round its chest. Nothing about it is decoration: its state shows four ways at once, all read from the building's one ranking (`src/shared/attention.ts`), so it agrees with the top bar, the Units rail, the Attention board and Mission control.

| State | Band | Ring on the floor | Glyph over it | Where it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Needs you | Signal orange | orange, a pulse spreading from it every 1.2 s | solid diamond | on its pod's ready line, facing the table, with a faint orange shaft over it |
| Stuck | red, blinking at 0.5 Hz | red, with a hatched band inside it | hollow triangle with a bar | at its console, darker and slumped forward |
| To review | amber | amber outline | hollow circle with a dot | at its console, turned toward the Review bay |
| Working | steel, breathing | faint steel | none | at its console |
| Parked | dark | none | none | at its console or on the Standby bench; dimmer still when its process is asleep |
| Merged | violet, for 6 s once its pull request merges | violet | check in a square | at its console |

Over each unit is a callout with its **call sign**, the seat it holds: `A-03` is the third console of pod A, `O-` the overflow bay, `S-` the Standby bench, `L-` a board agent's lectern and `R-` a chair in the Review bay (`src/shared/callsign.ts`, which the Units list and the Attention board use too). From across the deck the callout is one line, its state glyph, the call sign and the unit's name; near (or from twice as far for one that needs you or is stuck) it adds its task (its [tag] dropped, cut at a whole word) and how long it has been in its state. Callouts never cover each other (`src/client/features/workers/declutter.ts`): they are placed in the ranking's order, one in the way lifts a little, then shrinks to its call sign, and a working unit's with no room at all hides, while one that needs someone always shows. A unit whose callout is hidden shows its glyph alone, the same size on screen however far off it is. The provider's letter is on the visor (C Claude Code, X Codex, P Pi, CU Cursor) and a muted stripe in the provider's tint runs down its back.

The ready line keeps its order: a unit on it keeps its tick when the ranking moves, a new one takes the lowest free tick on its pod, a fifth one on a pod starts a second row, and a unit that has been answered holds its tick for 8 s before it glides back, so a state that flips back and forth doesn't send it to and fro (its ring changes color at once). Four lights hang over the four units most in need on the deck, orange or red: never one light per unit. `src/client/features/readyline/` does both, and `tests/units.test.ts` checks the ticks and the call signs.

A new unit builds up from its hover base in half a second, and one stood down goes dark, lifts off its pad and folds away into a line of light. Units glide, leaning a couple of degrees into the move; nothing hops, walks or bobs. Under reduced motion, glides and the build-up are cuts, the pulse is a still ring and the stuck blink is a steady band.

The people on the deck are **operators**: the same family at 1.75 m, with a narrow head plate and a visor slit, a light steel shell (so a person never reads as a unit) and a shoulder yoke in their own color, the one place it shows. While they talk the yoke's edge brightens and a mic tick lights beside their mono name plate. Settings > Your operator picks the shell's tone, the head plate and its tone, and the yoke.

## Walk and Overview

You arrive in **Walk**: first person, as before (third person is in Settings). Sitting in the captain's chair frames the bridge (`src/client/features/amphitheater/`): the field of view closes from 55 to 50 degrees and the view is aimed 30% of the way up the situation arc, so the bow and the arc fill the top half of the frame and the pit and the tiers with the crew the bottom half. You can still look anywhere, and the focus lean takes over once you move the mouse. On a page's first load the view comes in from outside: a 5 s arrival shot from ahead of the bow, past the destination world and down through the canopy to the conn; any key, click or mouse move lands you there at once, and it doesn't play with less motion or with a unit already waiting (see [the cinema](design.md#the-cinema)). With the Units rail open, the seated view is centred on the canvas right of it (`src/client/features/seatframe/`), so the arc's port wing is never under the rail. Press **G** for the **Overview**: the whole deck from above at 48 degrees, with no perspective, the shot to show someone the deck in one look. Each trip up starts 30 degrees off the axis on the starboard side, so the deck reads as a plan: the dais and the tiers near, the pit and the holo in the middle, the arc beyond with its boards turned toward you, fitted to the whole frame between the top bar and the bottom bar. From up there the hull and the floor are taken toward a dark slate (never a state's mark) and the light shafts are only a trace, so the units, the pods and their states stand out.

| Key | In the Overview |
| --- | --- |
| Q / E | Turn the deck a quarter (280 ms) |
| W A S D / arrows, or drag | Pan |
| Wheel | Zoom |
| G / Esc | Back to Walk |

A window opened from the Overview closes back to it with no extra click; one opened from Walk closes straight back to mouse-look. Going to a unit (N, a click on its toast, a search result) flies the view there in a 700 ms arc in Walk (`src/client/core/flight.ts`), or pans and zooms the Overview onto it in 300 ms (`flyTo` in `src/client/core/camera-overview.ts`). Under reduced motion, the turn and every flight are cuts. In demo mode (`?demo=1`) the Overview turns slowly round the table until you take over; see [docs/design.md](design.md#demo-mode).

## The merge beat

A merge is the one moment the deck celebrates, and it does it with light, not confetti. When a person merges a unit's pull request, a violet pulse runs from the unit's console to the mission table and its rim lights. When the bounty for it is released on devnet, the pulse runs on across the floor to the Proof corner and up the attestation rail, and parks as the rail's new lit segment; the vault's lid lifts with a violet glow, the top bar's violet counter rolls, and a proof toast shows the devnet transaction in mono with a settled tick and an explorer link. Then the bounty's coins fly out of the vault over the deck to the console of the unit that earned it, and a receipt floats up over the console for six seconds: the amount, the unit and its cell, the pull request and the devnet transaction. You hear them go: a tink as each leaves the vault, their rush across the deck, a tink as each lands and a soft chord ([Sound](design.md#sound)). An attestation landing on Base Sepolia plays the merged cue and a toast of its own, and a unit's first ERC-8004 record makes the plinth glow as its step lights. A unit deployed to a console gets a shorter beat of its own: a steel trace from the table out to it. The camera never moves for a beat, and under reduced motion there is no traveling light: the rail and the lid change at once and the toast says the rest (`src/client/features/beats/`).

## The bridge

The room is the bridge of a starship, built round the same plan (`src/client/features/bridge/`, fixtures on the floor's plan like every other):

- **Viewports.** The bow is one band of glass 26 m wide along the north wall, 2.2 to 6.5 m up, behind and over the situation arc, the hull's three frames standing across it as heavy ribs; from the captain's chair the arc covers its foot and space shows over the arc through the canopy. Down the east and west walls each bay has a wide, low port at a seated eye's height with its ends rounded off, and a slim strip over it, clear of the Review bay, the violet rail and the docs rack. Every port has a bezel with a dim cyan line round the glass inside and a bright one outside, no mullions, and glass that thickens to a cold tint at a slant. Over the south curb is the aft glass. The openings are `WINDOWS` in `src/shared/layout.ts`; `tests/layout.test.ts` keeps them clear of what hangs on the walls.
- **The hull.** A frame up the walls under every rib of the canopy, each with a ship-cyan line down its face, a ship-cyan cove along the foot of the walls and another where the walls meet the canopy, washing down the top of the wall, and seams at the viewports' sills and heads.
- **The canopy.** Sixteen ribs from a halo ring 9 m over the table out to the tops of the walls, two rings of purlins, and dark glass between them.
- **Outside.** The ship the bridge is part of: plated walls, a sloped skirt from their feet to the hull's edge rising at the bow into a glacis, an armoured brow along their tops, a chamfered hull under the slab with its bow to the north, a lit edge round it, two nacelles aft with their drive glow and hot cores, and running lights (`features/bridge/skin.ts`): red to port and green to starboard for the Overview only, white strobes at the bow and nacelles.
- **Space.** Round the ship (`src/client/features/space/`): a sky with the galactic band and its dust lanes crossing the forward viewport on a slant, a teal and indigo nebula just right of the bow with a lobe of it out of each side's ports, its filaments, knots and dark lanes, and crisp stars, baked once into a cube at load; three layers of stars streaming aft past the glass as the ship makes way north; the galactic band tilted to cross the side ports too; a meteor every 20 to 40 s; and every few minutes a planet or moon across a side port (clouds, a lit limb, a ring round a gas giant), an asteroid field tumbling past, or a comet across the forward glass. The ship makes way faster with each merge in the last hour (0.4x to 1.6x) and holds station when no unit is deployed or all are parked, and the drive glow follows. A merge surges it: the stars streak for 1.4 s and the glass glints. A milestone done jumps it: the stars stretch toward the bow, the view widens a few degrees, a glint comes up over the glass (a third by Night), and the ship comes out in a new region of space. Ship motion at Off removes all streaming past the ports; Calm keeps the stars but drops the streaks. The sky is only ever seen through glass, so none of it sits behind a board's text, and it keeps to neutrals, blues, teals and ship-cyan, never a state's hue. Close by (`src/client/features/vista/`): sheets of gas and dust streaming past the side ports at four depths, a ringed gas giant out of the port side about a third of a port across, and the sun high over the bow with its flare, cut by the canopy's ribs as you move under them (see [the design system](design.md#space-outside-the-glass)).
- **The world outside** (`src/client/features/destination/`, `fleet/`, `sorties/`). Dead ahead in the canopy, the mission's destination: a bright point at the start that grows with real progress only (waypoints passed and the open one's issues closed) to a third of the forward view, with a mono band under it ("MAKING FOR AUTH REWRITE - WAYPOINT 2 OF 4 - 35%", and "BEHIND SCHEDULE: 4 DAYS" while the waypoint is overdue), markers astern for the waypoints passed, and orbit once every one is passed. Off the side ports, every other deck as an escort in a V, its class by its units, its ports lit per unit at work, its name on its flank, a needs-you diamond over its bridge while a unit there waits on someone (click the ship for the Decks lift). Round the hull, a fighter for each unit at work patrolling past its pod's side port; open pull requests hold on the picket ahead of the bow; a merged one's fighter runs home to the hangar as the merge beat fires; a unit that needs you or is stuck has its fighter drift dark outside the glass. See [docs/design.md](design.md#the-world-outside).
- **The crew** (`src/client/features/vesper/`, `crew/`, `droid/`). VESPER's lines ride at the head of the ticker's log under its name. Units wear their epithet on their callout up close and thin chevrons on the outside of the left shoulder. On the Proof corner's top step the unit of the watch stands as a still hologram in a cone of cool white light, a plaque over it with its call sign, epithet and record on the last watch. Bolt, the bridge droid, docks on a charger on the west wall south of the Proof corner, flies at walking pace over the units' heads, round the table and in through the Review bay's door. None of it is in the Overview. See [docs/design.md](design.md#the-crew).
- **Rituals** (`src/client/features/launch/`, `drive/`, `turnaround/`, where they stand in `src/shared/ritual-slots.ts`). The drive core rises out of the Deck lift's roof, framed by the aft glass: twelve rings round a shader core, a ring lit ship-cyan for each merge in the current run and today's best etched on it as a thin white line, its light running up the column at the ship's cruise speed. The fleet's eight-week tally hangs over the Services panel. The pit wall, the captain's turnaround clock, stands on a mast over the Review bay, high enough to clear the Issues panel from the conn; when a wait clears fast a hairline runs from the bay's door down the west aisle and along the aft of the deck to the lift. The start of watch's log is typed onto a flat panel high over the forward view, as if projected on the glass (a page panel, so no rib cuts it). None of it is in the Overview but the hairline. See [docs/design.md](design.md#rituals).
- **Forward displays.** Each board of the situation arc, and the capacity strip, sits in a graphite bezel with a ship-cyan hairline over and under it. The counts are the Attention board's own band, so nothing hangs over the arc to repeat them; the ticker runs along its top. Every board is laid out the same way (`src/client/features/boards/screen.ts`): its name big and white in its own title bar with its count at the right, then as many rows as the board's height takes (two on a wing's board, three on the Attention board, four at most), the most in need first, each with its state's stripe and glyph, a first line in 0.24 m type that reads from the conn (0.28 m on the Attention board, whose names are short) and a smaller second line for up close, cut with an ellipsis rather than run off, and "+N more" under them. Nothing else on the deck is drawn over a board's text: the holo's stars and its cone fade out where a board's face is on screen, and a unit's callout that would cover a board docks in a slot row under that board's lower bezel, still tied to its unit by its hairline (see [the design system](design.md#the-bridge-from-the-captains-chair)). The canvases have the panels' own proportions, are painted at 300 pixels a metre, and are filtered with mipmaps and the renderer's most anisotropy (`src/client/world/sharp.ts`), so a board seen at a slant stays sharp. The capacity strip is one line: its name and whether there's room, CPU and memory each as a percent on a bar, and the units against the limit.
- **Stations.** Each console has a hull fin at either end and a ship-cyan trace along the edge of its top where its unit's hands rest. The face of its pedestal, toward the table, is a station screen: its unit's state glyph in its own hue and shape on the left (a dash at work or standing by), and on the right a readout. A working station's readout runs ship-cyan bars that scroll and grow with its terminal's output, a faint scan line passes every 6 s, and three blinkers twinkle at its end on slow periods of their own (4.3, 6.1 and 8.9 s). A stuck station shows still red hatching, one to review a steady amber line, one standing by a dim dash and one steady blinker, an empty console nothing.
- **Life** (`src/client/features/life/`). A working unit wears ship-cyan whatever its output (its band, its visor and a soft halo on the floor that breathes faster the busier it is); its hands stay at the console and work it harder while its terminal prints, its head bobs a little, and motes of cyan rise off its station's screen, more the busier it is. Now and then a busy station sends a data pulse, a point of ship-cyan light in a low arc, from its hood to the holo table, where dashes run along the course to the ship. Round the plot a band of light lettering turns slowly and says where the ship is making for and how far it has come ("CAPTAIN, WE ARE 40% OF THE WAY TO AUTH REWRITE", issues closed of those linked to the active milestone, never a made-up figure), the waypoint, the issues still out and the units on it; a progress ring on the tabletop shows the same fraction. Over the Attention board a ticker hangs along the arc's top: the ship's clock ticking by the second and how long the deck has been under way (since its earliest working unit started, or "holding station"), then the deck's log, the timeline's latest events, running right to left.
- **The amphitheatre** (`src/client/features/amphitheater/`). A ship-cyan ring round the pit's edge. The tiers' tops are laid with the deck's own floor, their risers in console graphite, each with a lit ship-cyan lip along its nosing and a toe light along its foot; the aisle is a flight of eight solid steps from the pit to the dais's lip, a lit nosing on each; brass rails run up the aisle's high end, along the galleries' outer edges and the back tier's back. Built from the same heights walking reads, merged by material: a handful of draws.

The canopy, the aft glass, the ticker and the condition band over the arc are on a layer of their own that the walk camera sees and the Overview's doesn't, so the Overview still looks straight down into the room; nothing on that layer can be clicked. The holo plot turns slowly round the table (half a turn a minute) and stands still under reduced motion. The bridge's life gives way to attention: for 3 s after a unit starts needing you or gets stuck everything ambient dims to 40%, a pod with such a unit stays hushed (its stations' bars at 30%, no pulses, no blinkers) while it lasts, and the whole bridge runs a quarter quieter while anyone is waiting on you. Calm halves it; Off, or reduced motion, stills it, and the screens still read. Settings > Bridge > Life sets how much the bridge lives: Full, Calm (no salutes, hail lines or patrols) or Silent running (no ambient life, the stars at a crawl, the ticker paused), with a switch each for the destination, the fleet and the squadron. Settings > Bridge > Ship motion sets how space moves: Full, Calm (half speed, no flybys, meteors or streaks) or Off (still, with the rest of the deck, as reduced motion is; a waypoint then crossfades the view in 400 ms).

Ship-cyan (`#6FC3DF`, `#2C5E70` for anything that glows over an area) is the instruments' own color and the working units' accent, never an attention state: hairlines, small type, the holo and the units at work. A state's hue only ever shows on a unit, its ring and glyph, its callout, its station's screen, the ready line and the counts.

## Light and materials

The bridge has two lights, picked in Settings > Bridge > Bridge lights: **Night** (low light, the default while the system is dark) and **Day** (high light), or **Auto**, which follows the system's dark or light setting. The rig is one table, `LIGHT_MODES` in `src/client/features/lights/modes.ts`: a cool fill from the sky and the slate below, a key through the forward viewport from high over the bow that throws the only shadows, a fill from aft through the aft glass, two low rims from east and west that cut units and consoles out of the floor, a spot over each pod, one over the table and a ship-cyan uplight in the holo table (`features/lights/rig.ts`). A mode only retunes those lights and never adds or takes one away, since that would recompile every material. By night the key is a cool blue-indigo starlight from over the bow with rims low from either side, the fill from the sky is low so the hull stays dark, the pods' lamps are a warm white, and a glow (bloom, threshold 0.8) lifts what is brightest. By day the ship is in high orbit over a sunlit planet: a warm, hard sun key, a cool fill, and the hull, walls and floor a mid-dark plate (albedo about 0.3 at most: the sun lifts them rather than whitening them), the consoles go darker still into graphite blocks, units stay graphite, lettering on the walls and floor turns dark, and there is no glow. Brightness steps every light up or down by 12% a step, two steps either way; the boards, callouts and state marks give their own light, so they read the same at every step. Each unit's marks sit on a 6 cm disc of instrument black (`#101720`) and every callout on a 92% dark chip, so a state's hue reads at 4.5:1 or better in both modes (`tests/lights.test.ts`). The room never tints with a state, by day or by night. Each surface has one of four finishes (`FINISH` in `src/client/world/office/materials.ts`): brushed steel and rails at roughness 0.35, the consoles' glass tops at 0.1, wall and deck plate at 0.6, rubber at 0.85; the units keep their own two-tone shells with a rim in their state's colour. Every matte surface carries the trim atlas (`src/client/world/office/trim.ts`), one 1024 texture baked at start with the plating's seams, bolt rows, vents, dirt in the seams and wear along their edges, cast from the world's own axes, so it needs no uvs and no draws. The consoles, hoods, the Standby bench and the lecterns have rounded edges, and cable trays, conduits, clamps and ribs with lightening holes run along the east, west and north walls (`greebles.ts`, four instanced draws), with lit channels in the starlight's indigo along the walls and up each rib (two more). The floor's tiles each take a shade of their own, and its walkways (round the table outside the ready lines, and out along the aisles) are scuffed and polished, reflecting the room (`floor.ts`). There are soft contact shadows under what stands on the floor, and lit hairlines along the edges that have to read in a dark frame: the slab, the walls' tops, the table's rim, the lift's portal, the hull's frames and the viewports.

The air and the floor carry the light too (`src/client/features/atmos/`). Shafts of light, warm with the sun, hang under five of the canopy's panes (falling the way the key's shadows do) and in at each low side port, never over a station, with dust drifting only inside them; a haze lies low over the floor, under about a metre, tinted by the sky ahead; pools of light lie on the floor under the boards, round the holo table, on the table side of each console, along the cove and inside the side ports; and the table's spot throws the canopy's ribs across the table and the deck round it. Light from outside reaches the room through the rig it already has: the key and the fill take the hue of the sky ahead and abeam, a passing planet's colour comes in through its port on the rim from that side, a comet or a meteor drags a glint across everything glossy, and the jump's flash washes the floor and the units ship-cyan. At High the pit's polished floor and the centre aisle mirror the wall boards. None of it touches an attention mark (they are `fog: false` and carry their own light), and when a unit needs the captain or is stuck the shafts, the dust, the ribs and the planet's wash duck to 60% for 2.5 s and then stand at 85% while anyone waits; a dark ring round the waiting unit on screen picks it out ([the look](design.md#the-look-value-light-and-colour)).

What the surfaces reflect is image-based light (`src/client/features/ibl/`): a probe over the table sees only what gives light inside (the practicals, the boards, the strips, the holo) and is the environment of everything inside, and a second one sees only the sky and lights the hull's outside. The room's probe is captured once the floor arrives and again on a change of the lights, Brightness or the alert condition, a face a frame; it never holds the sky, so nothing on the deck reflects the moving stars. The droid's charger is a cradle on the sill of a west port, under the glass rather than in front of it.

Settings > Bridge > Quality sets how much the deck draws: **Auto** (the default), **Low**, **Medium** or **High** (`src/client/features/quality/`). Auto starts from the graphics the browser names: Apple silicon and discrete GPUs draw at High, Intel and other integrated ones at Medium, software renderers at Low. Once the deck has loaded it watches the frames, leaving out the few seconds after anything that hitches once (a Night and Day switch, a jump, a floor arriving, the tab coming back, a burst of terminal output). Frames that keep falling behind for ten seconds step it down one tier, never twice in a minute; half a minute with room to spare steps it back up. On Apple silicon and discrete GPUs it holds at Medium or better unless frames are very slow. A step down is kept for a reload in the same browser session, never longer than a day, so a slow minute never holds a fast machine at Low. Under the row a chip says what it draws at now, *Auto - running at High*, with its last step and why (*stepped to Medium at 14:02, slow frames*); the menu (Tab) has it on its Quality row under Deck (*Auto - Medium since 14:02, slow frames*), with *Try High* to forget a step down at once. A tier picked by hand stays put. High draws 1.5 pixels per CSS pixel, the full glow, a 2048 shadow map every frame, every shaft of light with 1,500 motes in them and the floor's mirror of the boards; Medium 1.25 pixels, the glow at half size, a 1024 shadow map 20 times a second and the canopy's shafts with 800 motes; Low one pixel, no glow, a 1024 shadow map drawn again only when someone moves, two of the three star layers, no room light, sky light or trim (a software renderer feels every lookup), and of the light round the deck only the haze and the pools. The canopy's ribs and the light from outside are drawn at Medium and High. Every tier draws the boards, the marks and the callouts the same; Medium and Low leave a unit's smallest parts (its stripe, chest mark and letters, its laptop's hairline) out from further off. Switching applies at once, without recompiling anything. The viewport glass is almost clear looking straight through and thickens to a cold tint at a slant. The units' shells catch a cool rim light at their edges, so they hold their silhouette against the dark consoles. There are no outlines, no plants and no model files; everything is built in code. The colors are DESIGN.md's 3D row.
